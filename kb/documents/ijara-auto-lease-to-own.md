# Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek — Lease-to-Own Auto Finance

Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek is Mal's Sharia-compliant lease-to-own auto finance product, originated, funded and serviced end-to-end in the Mal app. Under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek, Mal buys the vehicle the customer has selected, registers it in Mal's own name, leases it to the customer for an agreed rent, and transfers ownership to the customer at the end of the term under a separate undertaking. This document is the full product policy and covers: the lease structure and ownership position; why the transfer promise is a standalone undertaking; eligibility requirements and vehicle criteria; fixed and variable rent mechanics and repricing; worked rent and early-buyout examples; the application-to-delivery timeline; handover and condition expectations; maintenance responsibility split; servicing matters including travel outside the UAE, modifications and additional drivers; total loss, theft and Takaful claims; lease transfer; early payment and fees; end-of-term execution; repossession, death and incapacity; complaints and escalation; Sharia governance; a glossary; and frequently asked questions.

## Structure and Ownership

Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek is a lease, not a loan. When a customer's application is approved, Mal pays the dealer directly, registers the vehicle in Mal's name as owner, and only then leases the vehicle to the customer as lessee. Mal's income under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek is rent earned for granting the customer the use (usufruct) of an asset Mal actually owns and actually bears the ownership risk of — it is not a return on money advanced.

Because Mal owns the vehicle throughout the term, Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek makes Mal the party exposed to the asset: if the vehicle is destroyed Mal loses it, and if a structural defect makes it unusable Mal must remedy it or stop charging rent. That exposure is what makes the rent legitimate, and it is why the maintenance split below is a structural requirement of Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek rather than a commercial courtesy.

The customer is registered in the UAE traffic system as the authorised user of the vehicle for the duration of Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek, with Mal recorded as owner and a mortgage or ownership restriction noted against the plate until the term completes.

Mal offers Murabaha cost-plus sale finance for other assets, but vehicles are financed under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek because a Murabaha price is fixed for life, forcing five years of funding risk into one number on day one, and because a depreciating, heavily used asset is better carried by the owner.

| Contract type                    | Cost-plus sale | Lease plus separate transfer undertaking     |

## Why the Transfer Promise Is a Separate Undertaking

Ownership at the end of Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek transfers under a separate, unilateral undertaking (wa'd) signed alongside the lease — either a promise of gift (hiba) or a promise to sell the vehicle for a token price, typically AED 100. It is deliberately a standalone document and never a clause inside the lease contract itself.

The reason is that a single contract cannot be two contracts at once. If the lease itself said "and at the end, ownership passes to the customer," the arrangement would be simultaneously a lease and a sale of the same asset between the same parties, with the sale contingent on a future event. Classical Sharia rules prohibit that combination, and the contingency also makes the subject matter of the sale uncertain at the time of contracting.

Keeping them separate preserves the integrity of both. During the term of Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek there is one live contract — a lease — and Mal bears owner's risk throughout. The transfer is a promise Mal has bound itself to and the customer has not paid for, executed by a distinct gift deed or token sale at maturity. Mal's Internal Sharia Supervision Committee treats this separation as a non-negotiable feature of Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek.

## Eligibility and Limits

Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek is available to UAE residents and nationals aged 21 to 65 at maturity, with a minimum monthly income of AED 8,000 for salaried applicants and AED 15,000 for self-employed applicants. Applicants must hold a valid UAE driving licence and a Mal account.

- Down payment: minimum 20% of the vehicle price for new vehicles, 25% for used vehicles.
- Maximum finance amount: AED 600,000 per customer under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek.
- Tenors: 12 to 60 months, in 12-month increments.
- Total monthly obligations, including rent under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek, must not push the customer's debt burden ratio above 50% of monthly income, in line with UAE Central Bank retail finance regulations.
- Applications are decided in the Mal app, typically within four working hours during business days.

## Vehicle Eligibility

Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek finances passenger vehicles only, subject to the following:

- New vehicles from any manufacturer with an authorised UAE dealer network.
- Used vehicles up to 5 years old at origination, and no more than 8 years old at the end of the lease term.
- Vehicles must be GCC-specification and registered in the UAE.
- Salvage-title, previously written-off, heavily modified and grey-import vehicles are not eligible for Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek.
- Commercial use — including ride-hailing, delivery, and rental — requires prior written approval from Mal, since it changes both the wear profile and the Takaful cover Mal must maintain as owner. Undisclosed commercial use is a breach of the lease.

## Rent Components and Repricing

Rent under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek is split into two portions, both disclosed on the app's rent schedule before the customer signs:

- **Fixed rent** — a fixed monthly amount covering the amortisation of Mal's acquisition cost across the term. This portion never changes.
- **Variable rent** — a floating portion recalculated periodically against a published benchmark, representing the market return on the asset Mal has tied up.

The variable portion of Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek is repriced every six months, on 1 January and 1 July, against the six-month Emirates Interbank Reference Rate as published two business days before the reset date, plus a fixed margin set at origination and disclosed in the offer letter. Mal caps any single reset movement at 150 basis points up or down, and caps total variable rent movement across the life of the contract at 400 basis points from the origination level. Customers receive an in-app notification with the new monthly rent at least fifteen days before a reset takes effect.

Repricing rent is not interest, because what is repriced is the price of using a physical asset, not the cost of borrowed money. The benchmark is only an external, transparent yardstick for what use of the vehicle is worth — Mal lends nothing under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek, and rent is payable only for periods in which the customer actually has the vehicle available to use.

## Worked Example: A 48-Month Rent Schedule

This example shows how rent is built under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek for a new vehicle priced at AED 120,000 over 48 months, with the minimum 20% down payment for a new vehicle.

- Vehicle price: AED 120,000
- Down payment paid by the customer to the dealer: AED 24,000 (20%)
- Mal's acquisition cost, paid directly to the dealer: AED 96,000
- Fixed rent: AED 96,000 ÷ 48 = **AED 2,000 per month**, unchanged for the whole term

Variable rent is calculated on Mal's outstanding acquisition cost at the start of each six-month rent period. At origination the six-month benchmark is 4.25% and the customer's margin is 2.25%, giving 6.50% per annum on AED 96,000, or AED 520 per month. At the following 1 January reset the benchmark moves to 5.00%, so the applied rate becomes 7.25% — a 75 basis point movement, inside the 150 basis point per-reset cap — while twelve fixed payments have cut the outstanding cost to AED 84,000.

| Rent period               | Outstanding cost | Applied rate | Fixed rent   | Variable rent | Monthly rent     |
| ------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------ | ------------ | ------------- | ---------------- |
| Months 1-6, before reset  | AED 96,000       | 6.50%        | AED 2,000.00 | AED 520.00    | **AED 2,520.00** |
| Months 13-18, after reset | AED 84,000       | 7.25%        | AED 2,000.00 | AED 507.50    | **AED 2,507.50** |

Monthly rent fell despite the benchmark rising, because the amortising cost shrank faster than the rate rose. The customer is notified fifteen days before the new figure applies.

## Worked Example: Early Buyout at Month 30

Continuing the same AED 120,000 vehicle under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek, the customer requests an early buyout quote in the Mal app during month 30 of the 48-month term. Buyout is available from month six onwards, so the request is in order.

- Fixed rent paid to date: 30 × AED 2,000 = AED 60,000
- Mal's outstanding acquisition cost: AED 96,000 − AED 60,000 = **AED 36,000**
- Accrued unpaid rent for the current month: AED 2,507.50
- Early buyout quote fee: AED 100
- Discretionary rebate granted by Mal: −AED 1,200
- Token transfer price under the transfer undertaking: AED 100

**Final settlement figure: AED 37,507.50**, valid for seven days from the date the quote is issued.

The AED 1,200 rebate is granted at Mal's sole discretion at settlement. It is not shown in the rent schedule, not promised at origination, and no customer settling Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek early has any contractual right to a rebate — if it were fixed in advance, remaining rent would behave like discountable debt and the lease would take on the character of a loan. Note what is absent: no rent for months 31 to 48 is charged, because the lease ends on settlement. On cleared funds Mal executes the transfer undertaking, releases the ownership restriction and registers the vehicle in the customer's name, normally within five working days.

## Early Buyout

A customer may buy the vehicle outright at any point after the sixth month of Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek by requesting an early buyout quote in the app. The quote is valid for seven days and states Mal's outstanding acquisition cost, any accrued unpaid rent, and the resulting settlement figure.

Mal may, at its sole discretion, grant a rebate on the remaining rent when a customer settles Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek early. The rebate is never a contractual entitlement, never promised at origination, and never shown in the rent schedule — if it were fixed in advance, future rent would effectively become a discountable debt and the lease would take on the character of a loan.

On settlement, Mal executes the transfer undertaking, releases the ownership restriction, and registers the vehicle in the customer's name, normally within five working days of cleared funds.

## Application to Delivery: The Full Timeline

Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek runs to a documented timeline from application to keys, measured in working days from a complete in-app application.

- **Day 0** — the customer applies in the Mal app and uploads Emirates ID (format `784-YYYY-NNNNNNN-N`), UAE driving licence, salary certificate or trade licence, and three months of bank statements. Mal runs affordability and the 50% debt burden ratio check.
- **Day 0, within four working hours** — credit decision issued in the app with the approved ceiling, tenor options and indicative rent.
- **Day 1** — the customer uploads a formal dealer quotation made out to Mal, with chassis number, specification and out-the-door price. Used vehicles also go for third-party valuation.
- **Day 2** — Mal issues the offer letter and rent schedule; the customer signs the lease and the separate transfer undertaking electronically and pays the down payment to the dealer.

- **Days 4-5** — registration with Mal as owner, the customer as authorised user, and an ownership restriction noted against the plate. Mal's Takaful cover incepts on the registration date.
- **Day 6** — handover, joint condition report, keys released, first rent due the same calendar day next month.

New vehicles typically complete in six working days; used vehicles add two to three days for valuation and inspection.

## Vehicle Handover, Condition Report and Mileage

At handover under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek the customer and the dealer complete a joint condition report in the Mal app before keys are released. It records the odometer reading, exterior panel condition, tyre tread depths, interior condition, the presence of both keys, the spare wheel and tool kit, and any pre-existing marks. At least twelve timestamped photographs are required — four corners, all panels, interior, dashboard and odometer — and the customer countersigns in the app before release.

The report matters because Mal owns the vehicle. It is the agreed baseline for any mid-term inspection, for end of term, and for an early buyout or lease transfer. Without it, a dispute at maturity about a pre-existing dent has no evidence on either side.

**Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek imposes no mileage cap.** Unlike an operating lease, the customer is expected to own the vehicle at maturity, so there is no residual value for Mal to protect and no excess-mileage charge exists at any tenor.

The condition standard at any inspection is that of a well-maintained vehicle of its age and mileage: scheduled servicing performed and documented, no unrepaired accident damage, no unapproved modifications, all original components present. Ordinary wear — stone chips, minor scuffs, faded trim, tyres within legal tread — is never charged.

## Maintenance Responsibility Split

The maintenance split is the operational core of Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek. As owner and lessor, Mal bears all major and structural maintenance, comprehensive Takaful cover, and every ownership-related registration cost. As lessee, the customer bears routine operating costs and ordinary wear and tear arising from their own use of the vehicle.

This is not a negotiated allocation. Under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek, costs that attach to owning the asset must sit with the owner — an owner who has shifted all asset risk to the lessee is not really an owner, and the rent would not be valid rent. Costs that attach to using the asset sit with the user.

| Cost item                                               | Who pays under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Engine, gearbox and drivetrain replacement or rebuild   | Mal (lessor)                                 |
| Structural, chassis or major electrical system repair   | Mal (lessor)                                 |
| Manufacturer recall work not covered by warranty        | Mal (lessor)                                 |
| Comprehensive Takaful premium                           | Mal (lessor)                                 |
| Annual vehicle registration and ownership transfer fees | Mal (lessor)                                 |
| Mandatory annual technical inspection fee               | Mal (lessor)                                 |
| Fuel and charging                                       | Customer (lessee)                            |
| Scheduled servicing, oil, filters, fluids               | Customer (lessee)                            |
| Tyres, brake pads, wipers, battery replacement          | Customer (lessee)                            |
| Minor repairs, dents, scratches and interior damage     | Customer (lessee)                            |
| Traffic fines and impound release charges               | Customer (lessee)                            |
| Salik toll charges and parking                          | Customer (lessee)                            |
| Car wash, detailing and accessories                     | Customer (lessee)                            |
| Takaful excess on a claim caused by customer negligence | Customer (lessee)                            |

Customers may use any workshop for items they bear. For any item Mal bears under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek, the customer raises a claim in the app and Mal settles directly with an approved workshop — the customer is never asked to pay and reclaim.

## Driving the Vehicle Outside the UAE

Because Mal is the registered owner of any vehicle financed under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek, the customer cannot cross a UAE border without an owner's No Objection Certificate issued by Mal. GCC border authorities require a bilingual NOC from the registered owner wherever the driver is not the owner, and a vehicle presented without one is turned back.

- Request the NOC in the Mal app with destination countries and travel dates, at least **five working days** before travel.
- Mal issues a bilingual Arabic/English NOC valid for up to 30 consecutive days, naming the destinations and the travel window.
- Fee: AED 100 per certificate, waived for the first NOC in each twelve-month period of the lease.
- NOCs are issued for GCC states only — Saudi Arabia, Oman, Bahrain, Kuwait and Qatar. Mal issues none for travel beyond the GCC.
- An NOC is refused while rent is in arrears, while an unresolved Takaful claim is open, or where a valid Emirates ID or licence is not on file.

Mal's Takaful cover is territorially limited to the UAE and Oman as standard. For any other GCC state the customer buys an orange-card border extension and uploads it to the app; Mal reimburses the cost on production of the receipt, since Takaful is an owner's cost under the Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek maintenance split. Driving outside the covered territory without an extension is prohibited use and voids Mal's shortfall protection on total loss.

## Modifications, Additional Drivers and Sub-leasing

The vehicle is Mal's property throughout Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek, so what may be done to it and who may drive it are governed by the lease rather than customer preference.

No approval is needed for dash cameras, phone mounts, seat covers, floor mats, roof boxes or any reversible accessory leaving no permanent trace. Prior written approval through the app — answered within three working days — is required for window tinting, tow bars, fixed roof racks, upgraded audio, alloy wheel changes and paint protection film. Tint is approved only within the UAE legal limit of 50% on side and rear glass, never on the windscreen, and a police tint permit must be uploaded. Prohibited outright: engine or ECU remapping, suspension or ride-height changes, exhaust modification, structural cutting or welding, and respraying in another colour. Approved modifications must be reversed at the customer's cost before an early buyout inspection or an end-of-term return.

Additional drivers must be registered in the Mal app with licence details before driving. Registration is free, immediate and open to up to four drivers per contract. An unregistered driver doubles the Takaful excess to AED 2,000 and voids cover entirely where they were not licensed for the vehicle class.

Sub-leasing is absolutely prohibited under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek: the customer holds usufruct granted by Mal for their own use and has no title to grant onward use for a consideration. Renting the vehicle out, listing it on a car-sharing platform, or using it for ride-hailing or delivery without Mal's written approval is a material breach entitling Mal to terminate and recover the vehicle.

## Total Loss and Theft of the Vehicle

If a vehicle financed under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek is written off, destroyed, or stolen and not recovered, the lease terminates on the date the vehicle stops being usable. It does not continue in the background while a claim is processed.

Mal, as owner and the party that holds the comprehensive Takaful cover, receives and applies the Takaful proceeds against Mal's outstanding acquisition cost. Rent already accrued and unpaid for periods before the loss remains due. Rent for every period after the loss is cancelled outright, because the customer no longer has the vehicle.

The Sharia reasoning is direct: rent under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek is consideration for usufruct actually received, so where there is no usufruct there is no rent. Charging for months in which the customer cannot use the vehicle would convert the rent into a debt repayment and defeat the structure. If Takaful proceeds exceed Mal's outstanding cost, Mal returns the surplus to the customer within twenty business days. If the proceeds fall short through no fault of the customer, Mal absorbs the shortfall as owner. The customer remains liable only where the loss resulted from their own gross negligence, prohibited use, or a breach of the Takaful terms.

## Making a Takaful Claim

Comprehensive Takaful cover on a vehicle financed under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek is held by Mal as owner, and Mal is the named participant on the certificate. The customer is the registered user and initiates any claim on Mal's behalf in the Mal app.

After an accident the customer must, in order: obtain a UAE police report (mandatory for every claim, no exceptions), photograph the damage and the scene, and open a claim in the Mal app within 48 hours. Mal's motor claims desk acknowledges within four working hours and assigns an approved workshop within one working day.

- Excess for a registered driver aged 25 or over: AED 1,000 per incident.
- Excess for a driver under 25, or any unregistered driver: AED 2,000 per incident.
- No excess is payable by the customer where the police report attributes fault to a third party.
- The excess sits with the customer where the incident arises from their negligence, prohibited use or an unregistered driver.

Where repairs are expected to exceed ten working days, Mal provides a courtesy vehicle of equivalent segment free of charge from day eleven. Rent under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek continues while a courtesy vehicle is provided, because usufruct is still being delivered; if none is available, rent is suspended day-for-day until the customer has a usable vehicle again.

## Transferring Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek to Another Customer

A live Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek contract may be transferred to another person by novation, most often when a customer leaves the UAE or passes the vehicle to a family member. The incoming customer takes over the lease and the transfer undertaking on the same terms, tenor and remaining balance, and the outgoing customer is fully released.

- The incoming customer must independently meet every eligibility rule: age 21 to 65 at maturity, income of AED 8,000 salaried or AED 15,000 self-employed, debt burden ratio at or below 50% including the transferred rent, a valid UAE licence and a Mal account.
- Rent must be fully up to date; a contract in arrears cannot be transferred.
- Lease transfer fee: AED 1,000, payable by the incoming customer.
- A joint inspection is run against the original handover report, and unapproved modifications must be reversed first.
- Mal decides within five working days; novation, re-registration and the updated undertaking complete within a further five.

The Sharia rationale is that a lease conveys usufruct, and usufruct is a transferable right — a lessee may assign the balance of a lease with the lessor's consent, because what passes on is a defined existing benefit, not a debt sold at a discount. Mal's consent is required because Mal remains the owner. The original origination margin carries across unchanged.

## End of Term: Executing the Transfer Undertaking

When the final rent payment under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek clears, Mal executes the separate transfer undertaking and the customer becomes legal owner. The sequence runs in the app.

- **Day 0** — final rent clears; Mal confirms zero outstanding rent, zero unpaid late payment charges and zero unsettled traffic fines against the plate. Fines must be cleared by the customer first.
- **Day 1** — Mal issues the executed transfer instrument: a gift deed (hiba) for no consideration, or a token sale at AED 100, depending on which form was signed at origination.
- **Days 2-4** — Mal releases the ownership restriction and lodges the ownership transfer with the UAE traffic authority.
- **Day 5** — registration is reissued in the customer's name. Mal's Takaful certificate is cancelled on the transfer date and the customer's own cover must incept the same day.

Within ten working days the customer receives, in the app and by email, the executed gift deed or token sale contract, a clearance letter confirming Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek is discharged, the ownership restriction release, the updated registration card, the full rent payment history and the original handover condition report. Mal charges no settlement, closure or documentation fee at maturity. The clearance letter should be retained, as it evidences discharge if a historical restriction query ever arises.

## If the Customer Does Not Want the Vehicle at Maturity

The transfer undertaking in Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek binds Mal, not the customer. Mal has promised to transfer ownership; the customer never paid for that promise and is free to decline it, by notifying Mal in the app at least 30 days before the final rent date.

On the agreed return date the customer delivers the vehicle to a Mal-appointed inspection centre with both keys, the spare wheel, the tool kit and the service history. A joint inspection runs against the original handover condition report. Ordinary wear and tear consistent with the vehicle's age and mileage is never charged, and because Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek has no mileage cap, high mileage alone is never a return charge. The customer is charged only for unrepaired accident damage, missing components, unapproved modifications not reversed, and a valeting charge of AED 300 where the vehicle arrives in a state that prevents inspection.

Mal then sells the vehicle. Because the customer paid rent for use and never contracted to buy, the customer has no claim on the proceeds and no liability if the vehicle sells for less than Mal expected — that is Mal's residual risk as owner. Rent up to the return date remains due in full, and the AED 100 token price is not charged where the transfer is declined.

## Repossession, Death and Incapacity

Mal may recover a vehicle financed under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek because Mal owns it, but ownership does not make recovery discretionary. Repossession follows sustained non-payment under Mal's Late Payment and Charity Policy and requires rent overdue by 90 calendar days or more, at least three documented contact attempts across app, SMS and telephone, and a final written notice giving 15 calendar days to remedy. A customer who clears arrears before recovery keeps the vehicle and the contract continues unchanged. Mal never uses third-party enforcement agents and never recovers without prior written notice.

Once recovered, the vehicle is independently valued and sold at market. Proceeds cover Mal's outstanding acquisition cost, then rent accrued and unpaid to the recovery date, then documented recovery and sale costs. **Any surplus is returned to the customer within twenty business days.** Mal may not recover more than is due, and never claims rent for months after recovery, because no usufruct is delivered for them.

On death or certified total permanent incapacity, Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek does not terminate automatically. The estate or an eligible heir may continue the lease on existing terms subject to standard eligibility, or terminate it by returning the vehicle with no early termination charge. Where the optional Takaful-based protection benefit is held, the outstanding acquisition cost is waived and Mal executes the transfer undertaking to the estate at no charge. Mal suspends collections for 60 calendar days from notification.

## Glossary of Terms Used in This Policy

- **Ijara** — a lease: one party grants another the use of an identified asset for a period in exchange for rent, while retaining ownership.
- **Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek** — literally "a lease ending in ownership": an ijara paired with a separate undertaking to transfer the asset to the lessee at the end of the term. This is the structure of Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek.
- **Wa'd** — a unilateral binding promise. Mal's promise to transfer the vehicle at maturity is a wa'd, kept outside the lease so the two are never one contract.
- **Hiba** — a gift; one of the two forms Mal's transfer undertaking may take, passing the vehicle for no consideration at maturity.
- **Usufruct (manfa'a)** — the right to use and benefit from an asset without owning it. Rent under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek is consideration for usufruct, which is why rent stops when the vehicle becomes unusable.
- **Takaful** — Sharia-compliant cooperative insurance, where participants contribute to a mutual fund that indemnifies members rather than buying risk transfer from a commercial insurer.
- **Lessor** — the owner granting the lease. Mal is the lessor and bears every ownership cost.
- **Lessee** — the party granted use of the asset. The customer is the lessee and bears operating and wear-and-tear costs.
- **Novation** — replacing one party to a contract with another by agreement, so the incoming party takes over the original terms and the outgoing party is released.
- **DBR (debt burden ratio)** — total monthly debt obligations as a percentage of monthly income; Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek requires 50% or below.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Who pays for new tyres on a vehicle financed under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek?**
The customer pays. Tyres are wear-and-tear items arising from the customer's own driving, so they sit on the lessee side of the Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek maintenance split, along with fuel, scheduled servicing, brake pads, batteries, minor repairs, traffic fines and Salik.

**Who pays for the comprehensive Takaful and a gearbox replacement?**
Mal pays for both. Comprehensive Takaful and major or structural maintenance — including engine, gearbox and drivetrain work — are ownership costs, and Mal is the legal owner of the vehicle throughout Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek. Mal also pays annual registration and ownership-related fees.

**If my financed vehicle is stolen and never recovered, do I keep paying rent?**
No. Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek terminates on the date the vehicle becomes unusable. Rent accrued before that date remains payable; all rent after it is cancelled, because rent is due only for usufruct actually received. Takaful proceeds are applied against Mal's outstanding cost, any surplus is returned to the customer, and any shortfall is absorbed by Mal unless the loss was caused by gross negligence or prohibited use.

**Why does my monthly rent change during the term?**
Because Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek has a fixed rent portion and a variable rent portion. The variable portion reprices every six months, on 1 January and 1 July, against the six-month Emirates Interbank Reference Rate plus your fixed origination margin, subject to a 150 basis point cap per reset and a 400 basis point cap over the life of the contract. Mal notifies you in the app at least fifteen days before any change.

**Is repricing the rent the same as charging interest?**
No. Under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek, Mal owns the vehicle and charges rent for its use. The benchmark is only a transparent external reference for what that use is worth, in the same way a landlord may review rent against a market index. No money is lent, and rent stops entirely if the customer can no longer use the vehicle.

**Can I settle early and own the car sooner, and will I get a discount?**
Yes, from month six onwards you can request an early buyout quote in the Mal app. The quote covers Mal's outstanding acquisition cost plus any accrued unpaid rent. Mal may grant a discretionary rebate on the remaining rent, but this is never guaranteed or contractually fixed in advance under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek. Once settled, Mal executes the transfer undertaking and registers the vehicle in your name, normally within five working days.

**Is there a mileage limit on Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek?**
No. There is no mileage cap and no excess-mileage charge at any tenor, because you are expected to own the vehicle at maturity and there is no residual value for Mal to protect. High mileage alone is never charged, even if you later decline the transfer and return the vehicle.

**Can I drive the car to Saudi Arabia or Oman?**
Yes, with an owner's No Objection Certificate from Mal, since Mal is the registered owner. Request it in the app at least five working days before travel; the bilingual NOC is valid up to 30 days, for GCC states only, at AED 100 with the first each year free. Mal's Takaful covers the UAE and Oman as standard — for Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Kuwait or Qatar you buy an orange-card extension at the border and Mal reimburses it.

**Can I tint the windows or fit a tow bar?**
Yes, with Mal's prior written approval in the app, answered within three working days, because the vehicle is Mal's property. Tint is allowed only within the UAE legal limit of 50% on side and rear glass, never the windscreen, with a police tint permit uploaded. Engine or ECU remapping, suspension changes, exhaust modification and respraying are prohibited outright under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek.

**Can someone else drive my financed vehicle?**
Yes, if they are registered as an additional driver in the Mal app first. Registration is free and immediate for up to four drivers per contract. An unregistered driver doubles the Takaful excess to AED 2,000 and voids cover entirely if not licensed for the vehicle class. Sub-leasing, renting the vehicle out or listing it on a car-sharing platform is absolutely prohibited.

**Who makes the Takaful claim after an accident, me or Mal?**
Mal holds the certificate as owner, but you initiate the claim in the Mal app on Mal's behalf, within 48 hours and with a mandatory UAE police report. Mal acknowledges within four working hours and assigns an approved workshop within one working day. The excess is AED 1,000 for registered drivers aged 25 and over, and nil where the police report puts fault on a third party. From day eleven of any repair Mal provides a free courtesy vehicle.

**What actually happens at the end of the term?**
When your final rent clears, Mal executes the separate transfer undertaking — a gift deed or a token sale at AED 100 — releases the ownership restriction and registers the vehicle in your name, typically within five working days. Outstanding traffic fines must be cleared first. Within ten working days you receive the executed transfer instrument, a clearance letter, the updated registration card and your full rent history. Mal charges no closure or documentation fee.

**Can Mal repossess the vehicle, and what happens to any surplus?**
Yes, because Mal owns it, but only after rent is 90 calendar days overdue, three documented contact attempts, and a final written notice giving 15 calendar days to remedy. Clearing arrears before recovery stops the process. The vehicle is then independently valued and sold; proceeds cover Mal's outstanding acquisition cost, unpaid accrued rent and documented recovery costs, and any surplus returns to you within twenty business days. Mal never claims rent for months after recovery.

# Mal Everyday Murabaha — Goods & Travel Finance Policy

Mal Everyday Murabaha is the in-app cost-plus purchase facility that lets Mal customers finance goods and travel without interest: Mal buys a specific asset the customer has chosen, takes ownership of it, and resells it to the customer at acquisition cost plus a profit amount declared in dirhams and fixed for the life of the contract. It is a sale of an identified asset, never a cash advance, and Mal pays the supplier directly rather than disbursing funds to the customer. This document is the authoritative reference for Mal Everyday Murabaha and covers the contract structure and its Sharia basis, the step-by-step app flow, pricing and worked arithmetic including early settlement with ibra', eligibility, tiers, limits and the debt-burden-ratio test, eligible and ineligible asset categories, agency purchases where the customer buys on Mal's behalf, merchant onboarding, delivery, defects, returns, travel cancellations and refunds, optional Takaful cover, instalment mechanics and payment dates, permitted contract variations, treatment of death, disability and job loss, complaints and escalation, how Mal Everyday Murabaha differs from Mal Ijara and Mal Tawarruq, Sharia governance, a glossary and pointers to related Mal documents.

## What Mal Everyday Murabaha Is

Mal Everyday Murabaha is a cost-plus sale, not a loan. Mal buys a specific asset that the customer has chosen, takes ownership of it, and then sells that same asset to the customer at a price made up of Mal's acquisition cost plus a declared profit amount. The customer pays that total price in monthly instalments.

Because Mal Everyday Murabaha is a sale, there is no interest at any point. Mal does not lend cash and does not charge for the passage of time. Mal's return is a one-off profit amount agreed in the sale contract, disclosed in dirhams before the customer accepts, and fixed for the life of the contract.

Under Mal Everyday Murabaha the customer never receives money to spend at their discretion — Mal pays the supplier directly, and the customer receives the asset itself.

## How the Mal App Flow Works

1. The customer opens the Mal app, selects **Buy with Murabaha**, and chooses a specific item — a flight itinerary, a laptop, a phone, furniture, appliances or a school-fee invoice from a Mal partner merchant.
2. Mal runs an instant eligibility check against the customer's Mal profile, verified income and existing obligations, returning a decision in under 90 seconds for most applicants.
3. Mal issues a **Purchase Undertaking** — a promise by the customer to buy once Mal owns the asset. It is not itself the sale contract.
4. Mal purchases the asset from the merchant, paying the supplier directly, and takes ownership, bearing risk from purchase until the sale to the customer completes.
5. Mal offers the asset to the customer at a disclosed total price. The customer accepts in-app with a signed digital acceptance, and the Murabaha sale contract is formed at that moment.
6. The asset is delivered or the booking is ticketed, and instalments begin on the customer's next salary date.

Steps 4 and 5 must happen in that order and Mal's systems enforce the sequence: a Mal Everyday Murabaha contract signed before Mal holds title would be a disguised loan with a mark-up on money. Both timestamps appear on the customer's contract. If a laptop is damaged in transit before acceptance, the loss is Mal's and the customer owes nothing.

## Eligible Asset Categories

Mal Everyday Murabaha finances identifiable, lawful, deliverable assets, including:

- Air travel, hotel bookings and package holidays through Mal Travel partners
- Consumer electronics: laptops, phones, tablets, cameras
- Home furniture, white goods and air conditioning units
- Medical and dental procedures at partner clinics
- Education fees invoiced by a registered institution
- Vehicle accessories, tyres and servicing packages

## Ineligible Asset Categories

Mal Everyday Murabaha will decline, and Mal systems automatically block, the following:

- **Gold, silver and currency on deferred terms.** These are *ribawi* counter-values and must be exchanged spot, hand to hand. A deferred-payment sale of gold is not permitted under Mal Everyday Murabaha in any tenor.
- Alcohol, tobacco, vaping products, pork products and gambling of any kind
- Conventional financial products: interest-bearing loans, conventional insurance premiums, credit-card balance settlements, margin trading accounts
- Cash, gift cards, prepaid vouchers, cryptocurrency and any instrument redeemable for cash
- Adult entertainment, weapons and any purchase prohibited under UAE law
- Refinancing of an existing Mal Everyday Murabaha contract or of a third-party debt

Where a merchant basket mixes eligible and ineligible items, Mal Everyday Murabaha finances only the eligible portion and the customer pays the remainder from their Mal account balance.

## Eligibility and Documentation

To open a Mal Everyday Murabaha contract, a customer must:

- Be aged 21 or over
- Hold valid UAE residency or UAE nationality
- Hold a verified Emirates ID, checked through Mal's in-app identity verification at onboarding and re-verified at each Emirates ID renewal
- Receive salary into an account Mal can verify, with at least three months of history
- Meet the minimum salary for the requested tier and pass the debt-burden-ratio test
- Have no Mal Everyday Murabaha contract more than 60 days in arrears

Mal collects a salary certificate or three months of bank statements at onboarding, and does not re-request them for each Mal Everyday Murabaha transaction unless the requested amount exceeds AED 60,000 or the customer's declared employer has changed.

For each Mal Everyday Murabaha customer, Mal holds an account profile containing: full name, Emirates ID number, nationality and residency status, date of birth, registered mobile and email, employer and verified monthly salary, Mal account number and balance, tier, all open and settled Murabaha contracts with their asset descriptions and schedules, and the debt-burden-ratio calculation as at the last assessment.

Mal support agents and the Mal AI assistant can read a customer's own profile to answer questions about their Mal Everyday Murabaha limits, contracts and instalments. Identifiers are masked before any query reaches a language model.

## Financing Limits and Tenors

Mal applies an internal single-customer cap of **AED 150,000** across all open Mal Everyday Murabaha contracts, regardless of income. Individual transaction limits and tenors are set by tier:

| Tier | Min verified monthly salary | Max per transaction | Available tenors | Max open contracts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | AED 8,000 | AED 15,000 | 3, 6, 12 months | 2 |
| Core | AED 15,000 | AED 60,000 | 3, 6, 12, 24 months | 4 |
| Prime | AED 30,000 | AED 150,000 | 6, 12, 24, 36 months | 6 |

Mal also applies a debt-burden-ratio test in line with UAE Central Bank retail lending rules: total monthly instalments across all Mal and non-Mal obligations must not exceed **50%** of the customer's verified monthly income, and Mal declines any Mal Everyday Murabaha application that would breach it. Mal applies a stricter internal ceiling of 40% for Starter-tier customers.

The minimum Mal Everyday Murabaha transaction is AED 500. Tenors are fixed at contract and cannot be extended after acceptance.

## Fixed Total Price

The total price under a Mal Everyday Murabaha contract is locked at the moment of contract and never changes. It does not increase if the customer pays late, if the tenor is extended, if benchmark rates move, or if Mal's own funding costs rise.

Example: Mal buys a laptop for AED 6,000 and sells it to the customer for AED 6,540 — a declared profit of AED 540 over 12 months. The customer's obligation is AED 6,540 and nothing more, whether it is settled in month 6 or month 18.

Every Mal Everyday Murabaha contract shows, on one screen before acceptance: the asset cost, the profit amount in dirhams, the total sale price, the instalment amount, the number of instalments and the first payment date.

## Worked Example: A 12-Month Contract

A Core-tier customer selects a laptop priced at AED 12,000 from a Mal partner merchant on a 12-month tenor. The full arithmetic of the Mal Everyday Murabaha contract is:

- Asset cost to Mal, paid to the merchant: **AED 12,000.00**
- Declared Mal profit: **AED 1,080.00** (9.00% of cost, flat, for the whole 12 months)
- Total Murabaha sale price: **AED 13,080.00**
- Monthly instalment: AED 13,080.00 ÷ 12 = **AED 1,090.00**

Every instalment is identical and reduces the outstanding sale price by exactly AED 1,090.00. There is no interest component, so the schedule has no principal/profit split and nothing is recalculated if payment timing shifts.

| Point in contract | Instalments paid | Paid to date (AED) | Outstanding sale price (AED) |
|---|---|---|---|
| At acceptance | 0 | 0.00 | 13,080.00 |
| End of month 3 | 3 | 3,270.00 | 9,810.00 |
| End of month 6 | 6 | 6,540.00 | 6,540.00 |
| End of month 9 | 9 | 9,810.00 | 3,270.00 |
| End of month 12 | 12 | 13,080.00 | 0.00 |

The total obligation under this Mal Everyday Murabaha contract is AED 13,080.00 and cannot become any other number. Paying in month 14 rather than month 12 does not increase it.

## Instalment Mechanics and Payment Dates

Mal Everyday Murabaha instalments are collected by **direct debit from the customer's Mal account**, set up automatically at acceptance. No separate mandate form is required and there is no collection fee.

The first instalment falls on the customer's next verified salary date at least 7 calendar days after delivery or ticketing — so a laptop delivered on the 22nd with salary on the 25th produces a first instalment on the 25th of the following month. Later instalments fall on the same day each month; where that day does not exist in a shorter month, collection moves to the last day of that month.

A customer may **change the due date once per contract**, under **My Contracts → Payment date**, to any day from the 1st to the 28th, with at least 3 working days' notice and effect from the next unpaid instalment. It never changes the number of instalments, the instalment amount or the total sale price. If the employer changes the salary date, Mal detects the shift after two consecutive cycles and prompts a realignment that does not consume the once-per-contract change.

**Partial prepayment** is allowed any time in multiples of AED 100, minimum AED 500, and is applied to the final instalments — shortening the contract rather than reducing the monthly amount. Ibra' is granted only on full settlement.

## Early Settlement and Ibra'

A customer may settle a Mal Everyday Murabaha contract in full at any time through the app. The amount contractually owed on early settlement is the **full outstanding sale price** — the debt is a fixed sale receivable, not a balance that unwinds with time.

Mal may, at its **sole discretion**, grant a rebate on the unearned portion of its profit. This rebate is called *ibra'*. In current practice Mal grants ibra' of up to 60% of the remaining profit on Mal Everyday Murabaha contracts settled early, applied automatically at the settlement screen.

Ibra' cannot be written into the Mal Everyday Murabaha contract as a customer entitlement. If a rebate were a contractual right, the sale price would become variable and time-dependent, which would reintroduce the very interest-like uncertainty (*gharar* over the price) that the Murabaha structure exists to avoid. Mal therefore states its ibra' practice as policy, not as a term of sale, and the Mal Internal Sharia Supervision Committee reviews that practice annually.

There is no early settlement fee under Mal Everyday Murabaha.

## Worked Example: Early Settlement with Ibra'

Continuing the same Mal Everyday Murabaha contract — AED 12,000 asset cost, AED 1,080 profit, AED 13,080 total, AED 1,090 per month over 12 months — assume the customer decides to settle in full immediately after paying instalment 5.

Step 1 — outstanding sale price. Five instalments of AED 1,090.00 have been paid, totalling AED 5,450.00. The contractual amount owed is the remaining sale price: AED 13,080.00 − AED 5,450.00 = **AED 7,630.00**. That is the legally due figure, because a Murabaha debt is a fixed sale receivable and does not unwind with time.

Step 2 — unearned profit. Profit is treated as accruing evenly across the 12 instalments at AED 90.00 per instalment (AED 1,080 ÷ 12). Five instalments have run, so earned profit is AED 450.00 and remaining profit is **AED 630.00**.

Step 3 — ibra'. Mal currently grants ibra' of up to 60% of remaining profit, applied automatically at the settlement screen: AED 630.00 × 60% = **AED 378.00**.

Step 4 — final figure. AED 7,630.00 − AED 378.00 = **AED 7,252.00** payable today. There is no early settlement fee.

The settlement screen in the Mal app shows all four lines before the customer confirms, and the quote is valid for 48 hours. Ibra' remains discretionary, so the confirmed quote — not the contract — is what fixes the discounted amount.

## Late Payment

Mal Everyday Murabaha does not permit any increase in the total sale price for late payment. Missed instalments are handled under the separate **Mal Late Payment & Charity Policy**, which covers the reminder sequence, the late payment charity contribution, its calculation and independent disbursement, restructuring options, and credit bureau reporting. Customers and support staff should refer to that document directly; this policy does not restate its terms.

## Contract Variations After Acceptance

Once a Mal Everyday Murabaha sale is concluded, it is a completed sale of an identified asset at a stated price, and that fixes what may be varied afterwards.

**Cannot change under any circumstances:**

- The **total sale price**. A price that could move after acceptance would make the consideration uncertain (*gharar*) and turn a sale into a time-priced debt. This holds whether the change would favour Mal or the customer.
- The **tenor**. Extending the schedule at a fixed price is available only through the restructuring route in the Mal Late Payment & Charity Policy, a hardship remedy rather than a customer option.
- The **financed asset**, since a different item is a different sale requiring a new contract.
- The **profit amount**, which is a term of the concluded sale.

**Can change:** the instalment due date, once per contract; the funding account, where the customer holds more than one Mal account; optional Takaful, addable within 14 days of acceptance and cancellable at any time; the delivery address, up to dispatch; and the sale price **downwards only**, in the two narrow cases already defined — a confirmed defect settled by price reduction, and a partial refund on cancelled travel.

Customers wanting different terms settle the existing Mal Everyday Murabaha contract, with ibra' where granted, and take a new one. Mal does not refinance its own contracts, since refinancing a Murabaha debt is a debt-for-debt sale.

## Asset Delivery, Defects and the Right to Reject

Under Mal Everyday Murabaha the customer buys a real asset and therefore holds the classical buyer's protection known as *khiyar al-'ayb* — the option to reject goods for an undisclosed defect existing at the time of sale.

Mal gives effect to this with a **7 calendar day inspection window** for physical goods, running from confirmed delivery. Within that window the customer reports the defect under **My Contracts → Report a problem**, and Mal arranges collection at Mal's cost within 3 working days. The customer never has to deal with the merchant.

If the defect is confirmed, the Mal Everyday Murabaha sale is **rescinded**, not refinanced. Rescission unwinds the contract completely: the sale price is cancelled in full, instalments already collected are returned to the customer's Mal account within 5 working days, Mal retains no profit, and the contract leaves the customer's open-contract count and debt-burden-ratio calculation. Mal then pursues the merchant under the Mal Merchant Agreement; that dispute is Mal's.

Where the defect is minor and the customer prefers to keep the asset, Mal may agree a **price reduction**, lowering the total sale price and the remaining instalments proportionally. This is the one circumstance in which a Mal Everyday Murabaha sale price may fall after acceptance. Defects reported after the 7-day window fall to the manufacturer's or merchant's warranty, and instalments continue.

## Cancellations and Refunds on Travel Bookings

Travel is financed under Mal Everyday Murabaha exactly as goods are: Mal buys the ticket or booking and sells it on. Cancellation is therefore a partial or full unwind of a sale, not a refund of a loan. The customer cancels under **My Contracts → Travel → Cancel booking**, and Mal recovers whatever the fare rules allow.

- **Full refund from the carrier** (24-hour void window, or a flexible fare): the Mal Everyday Murabaha contract is rescinded, all instalments paid are returned within 5 working days, and Mal retains no profit.
- **Partial refund**: Mal reduces the sale price to the recovered amount plus the same profit *rate* applied to the retained cost, then reissues the schedule over the original remaining tenor. Mal never keeps profit on the cancelled portion.
- **No refund** (non-refundable fare, no-show): the asset was delivered as contracted, so the full sale price remains payable on the original schedule.
- **Airline-initiated cancellation**: treated as a full refund regardless of fare type, with Mal absorbing any shortfall.

Worked illustration: a AED 5,000 ticket sold at AED 5,400 (8% profit) over 6 months, cancelled after two instalments of AED 900. The carrier refunds AED 3,000, so retained cost is AED 2,000, profit on it AED 160, and the revised sale price AED 2,160. Against AED 1,800 already paid, AED 360 remains, collected as two instalments of AED 180. Carrier vouchers are credited at face value on the same basis.

## Takaful Cover on Financed Goods

Mal offers optional **Takaful** cover on high-value assets financed under Mal Everyday Murabaha. Takaful is cooperative mutual protection, in which participants contribute to a pooled fund used to compensate members who suffer loss; it is the permitted alternative to conventional insurance, avoided because of uncertainty (*gharar*) and interest in the premium pool. Cover is offered at checkout on Mal Everyday Murabaha contracts of **AED 20,000 or more**, underwritten by a UAE-licensed Takaful operator on Mal's panel, not by Mal itself.

- **Who pays**: the customer, as a separate monthly contribution shown on its own line and *never* added into the Murabaha sale price. Typical contribution is 0.35% of asset cost per year — about AED 8.75 a month on a AED 30,000 asset.
- **What it covers**: accidental damage, fire, theft with a police report, and total loss. On a valid total-loss claim the operator pays the outstanding sale price directly to Mal and the contract closes.
- **What it excludes**: wear and tear, cosmetic damage, travel bookings, deliberate damage, and assets outside the UAE for more than 90 consecutive days.

Takaful is **never a condition of approval** for Mal Everyday Murabaha. Mal does not price the contract differently, ask again after a decline, or let checkout default to acceptance. Cover can be cancelled any time, effective from the next contribution date, without affecting the contract.

## Agency Appointment: When the Customer Buys on Mal's Behalf

For most Mal Everyday Murabaha transactions Mal pays the partner merchant directly. Where a merchant is not integrated with Mal — a specialist supplier, an overseas retailer, a clinic outside the partner network — Mal may instead appoint the customer as its purchasing **agent** under a *wakala*. The customer buys the asset in Mal's name, it becomes Mal's property on purchase, and only afterwards does Mal sell it on under Mal Everyday Murabaha.

Agency is the weakest point in any Murabaha, because the same person is both Mal's buying agent and the eventual purchaser. Done loosely, the two legs collapse into one and the transaction becomes a cash advance with a mark-up. Mal therefore applies five controls:

1. The wakala appointment, the supplier purchase and the Murabaha acceptance carry three separate timestamps, all shown on the contract.
2. The supplier invoice must name Mal as buyer, with the Mal Everyday Murabaha reference number on its face.
3. Funds are released to the supplier's account, never to the customer's Mal account.
4. No self-dealing: no purchase from oneself, an employer one controls, or a first-degree relative.
5. Invoice and proof of delivery must be uploaded within 7 calendar days; agency transactions above AED 25,000 are reviewed by a Mal operations analyst before the sale leg is offered.

Agency purchases are capped at AED 40,000 per transaction.

## Merchant Partners and Onboarding

Mal Everyday Murabaha works best when Mal buys directly from an integrated merchant, so Mal maintains a partner network admitted through onboarding run by the Mal Merchant Partnerships team. Admission requires a valid UAE trade licence, at least 12 months of trading history, VAT registration where turnover requires one, verified beneficial ownership, and a signed Mal Merchant Agreement incorporating the Sharia schedule. Onboarding takes 10 working days from complete submission.

Screening for ineligible inventory is the substantive step. A merchant whose catalogue includes any category blocked under Mal Everyday Murabaha — alcohol, tobacco and vaping, gambling, pork products, adult goods, weapons, gold and silver bullion, gift cards and prepaid vouchers, or cryptocurrency — is either declined or admitted on a restricted SKU list enforced at checkout. Supermarkets and department stores are onboarded on restricted lists as a matter of course. Mal re-screens every partner catalogue quarterly and on any bulk SKU upload.

Settlement is net 3 working days from delivery confirmation for integrated partners and net 7 for restricted-list partners. Mal pays the full asset cost and takes no hidden merchant discount, because the disclosed cost in a Mal Everyday Murabaha contract must be Mal's true acquisition cost; any rebate Mal receives is deducted from the cost shown to the customer. Merchants breaching the Sharia schedule are suspended within one working day.

## Death, Disability and Loss of Employment

Mal Everyday Murabaha debt does not disappear on death, but Mal's handling is designed so that no family is pursued informally.

**Death.** On notification with a death certificate, Mal freezes the Mal Everyday Murabaha contract immediately: collections stop, no charity contribution accrues, and no relative is contacted about payment for 90 calendar days. The outstanding sale price becomes a claim against the estate, settled in estate administration alongside other debts and before distribution to heirs. Where the financed asset carried Mal Takaful cover, the operator settles the outstanding sale price directly. Where the estate is insufficient, Mal writes off Mal Everyday Murabaha balances under AED 25,000 rather than pursue heirs, and refers larger shortfalls to the Mal Credit Committee.

**Permanent total disability.** On accepted medical evidence the same 90-day freeze applies. Takaful, where in force, settles the contract; otherwise Mal offers a rescheduled plan at the same fixed total sale price.

**Loss of employment.** The customer notifies Mal in the app within 30 days. Mal applies a payment holiday of up to 3 months, during which no charity contribution accrues and no arrears reach the credit bureau. The total sale price is unchanged and the schedule extends by the holiday length. Beyond 3 months the case routes to the restructuring provisions of the Mal Late Payment & Charity Policy.

## Complaints, Disputes and Escalation

Any customer dissatisfied with a Mal Everyday Murabaha decision, charge, contract or service may complain, and Mal treats complaints as a governance input rather than a support ticket.

**Stage 1 — Mal complaints team.** Raise a complaint under **Help → Raise a complaint** or by email to support@mal.example. Mal acknowledges within 1 working day with a reference in the form `CMP-nnnnnn` and issues a substantive written response within **5 working days**, extendable to 10 where merchant or Takaful-operator input is needed, with written notice of any extension.

**Stage 2 — Mal internal review.** Within 30 calendar days of the Stage 1 outcome the customer may ask the Mal Customer Review Panel to review it. The panel is independent of the original decision-maker and responds within **10 working days**.

**Stage 3 — Sharia escalation.** Complaints touching the Sharia compliance of Mal Everyday Murabaha — ownership sequencing, ibra' practice, defective-asset treatment, agency arrangements — go to the Mal Internal Sharia Supervision Committee at issc@mal.example or sharia@mal.example, which issues a binding written determination within **20 working days**.

**Stage 4 — external.** If unresolved after 30 working days, or the customer rejects Mal's final response, the customer may escalate to the UAE Central Bank's consumer protection route, Sanadak. Mal includes those details in every final response and never asks a customer to waive that right.

## Choosing Between Mal Financing Products

Mal offers three distinct financing structures, and choosing correctly matters more than choosing cheaply. Mal Everyday Murabaha is the default for identifiable goods and travel; the others exist for cases it cannot serve.

| | Mal Everyday Murabaha | Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek | Mal Tawarruq |
|---|---|---|---|
| What you get | The asset itself, bought and resold to you | Use of the asset under a lease, title transferring at the end | Cash, after selling a commodity bought from Mal |
| Asset ownership | Passes to you at acceptance | Stays with Mal through the lease term | Commodity is yours briefly, then sold on |
| Price behaviour | Fixed total sale price, never varies | Rentals fixed or re-set at review points | Fixed total deferred price |
| Typical use | Electronics, furniture, flights, school fees, medical procedures | Vehicles and other high-value long-lived assets | Genuine cash needs only, with a stated purpose |
| Typical tenor | 3 to 36 months | 24 to 60 months | 6 to 24 months |

Practical routing: if the customer can name the item, Mal Everyday Murabaha is almost always the answer. If the item is a car, Mal Ijara is usually cheaper per month because Mal retains ownership and residual value. If the customer needs money rather than a thing, only Mal Tawarruq applies — and Mal declines it where Mal Everyday Murabaha could finance the underlying purchase directly.

## Tawarruq and Why Mal Everyday Murabaha Is Not a Cash Facility

Mal Everyday Murabaha never disburses cash. If a customer needs money rather than an asset, the only Mal product that can meet that need is **Mal Tawarruq**, a separate, tightly limited facility with its own contract and approval track.

Tawarruq works by sale, not lending: Mal buys a freely traded commodity — Mal uses London Metal Exchange aluminium warrants through two independent brokers — and sells it to the customer at cost plus profit on deferred terms. The customer then sells the commodity to an unrelated third party for spot cash, ending with cash and a fixed deferred debt.

Many scholars are uneasy with *organised* tawarruq, where the bank arranges both legs so the commodity never meaningfully moves and the transaction becomes lending in substance; the OIC Islamic Fiqh Academy has criticised that form. Mal's Internal Sharia Supervision Committee therefore requires that Mal never act as the customer's agent for the onward sale, that the buying and selling brokers be different entities, that warrant numbers appear on the contract, and that at least one hour separate the two legs.

Mal caps Mal Tawarruq at AED 75,000 per customer, restricts it to Core and Prime tiers, and requires a stated purpose. Mal declines a Tawarruq application where the stated purpose is a purchase Mal Everyday Murabaha could finance directly.

## Sharia Governance

Mal Everyday Murabaha is approved by the **Mal Internal Sharia Supervision Committee (ISSC)**, which reviews the product structure, the standard contract wording, the Purchase Undertaking, the ibra' policy and the merchant onboarding criteria at least annually.

The Mal Everyday Murabaha structure is aligned with AAOIFI Sharia Standard No. 8 (Murabaha) and Sharia Standard No. 9 (Ijarah, where a transaction is redirected to a lease structure). Mal's ownership-sequencing controls, ineligible-asset blocks and prohibition on price increases are the operational expression of those standards.

Any income Mal identifies as non-compliant — for example, profit on a contract where the ownership sequence was not correctly evidenced — is purified and disbursed to charity under ISSC supervision, and is not recognised as Mal revenue.

## Glossary of Terms Used in This Policy

- **Murabaha** — a cost-plus sale in which the seller discloses acquisition cost and the profit added to it. Mal Everyday Murabaha is a deferred-payment murabaha.
- **Ibra'** — a discretionary rebate of unearned profit on early settlement. It cannot be a contractual right, as that would make the sale price variable.
- **Wa'd** — a unilateral promise. The Purchase Undertaking in Mal Everyday Murabaha is a wa'd to buy once Mal owns the asset, not the sale itself.
- **Ribawi** — categories of goods (notably gold, silver and currency) that must be exchanged spot and like-for-like, and are therefore blocked under Mal Everyday Murabaha.
- **Khiyar al-'ayb** — the buyer's option to rescind a sale on discovering an undisclosed defect present at the time of sale.
- **Wakala** — an agency contract, used under Mal Everyday Murabaha when Mal appoints the customer to buy an asset on Mal's behalf.
- **Tawarruq** — buying a commodity on deferred terms and selling it on for spot cash. It underlies Mal Tawarruq and is never used in Mal Everyday Murabaha.
- **Gharar** — excessive uncertainty in a contract's subject matter or price. Fixing the Mal Everyday Murabaha sale price at acceptance is the main control against it.
- **DBR (debt-burden ratio)** — monthly debt instalments as a percentage of verified monthly income. Mal Everyday Murabaha applies a 50% ceiling, 40% for Starter tier.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Mal Everyday Murabaha a loan?**
No. It is a sale of a specific asset. Mal buys the item, owns it, and sells it to the customer at cost plus a declared profit. The customer never receives cash, and Mal never charges for the passage of time.

**Can the amount I owe go up?**
No. The total sale price under a Mal Everyday Murabaha contract is fixed at acceptance and cannot increase for any reason, including late payment, tenor changes or market rate movements.

**Why does Mal have to buy the item first?**
Because a seller cannot sell what the seller does not own. A Mal Everyday Murabaha contract signed before Mal held title would be a loan with a mark-up. Mal timestamps the supplier purchase and the customer acceptance separately, and both appear on the contract.

**Will I get a discount if I pay off my Mal Everyday Murabaha early?**
Usually, but it is not a right. You owe the full outstanding sale price. Mal may grant *ibra'* on unearned profit at its sole discretion, currently up to 60% of remaining profit. Making it contractual would render the sale price variable and time-dependent.

**Can I use Mal Everyday Murabaha to buy gold?**
No. Gold, silver and currency cannot be sold on deferred terms, so Mal Everyday Murabaha blocks them at any amount or tenor, as it does cash, gift cards, prepaid vouchers and cryptocurrency.

**How much can I finance?**
Up to AED 150,000 across all open Mal Everyday Murabaha contracts, subject to your tier limit — AED 15,000 per transaction on Starter, AED 60,000 on Core, AED 150,000 on Prime — and a debt-burden ratio ceiling of 50% of verified monthly income.

**What happens if I miss an instalment?**
The amount you owe does not increase. Missed instalments are handled under the separate Mal Late Payment & Charity Policy, which covers reminders, the charity contribution, restructuring and credit reporting.

**How much would I actually pay on a AED 12,000 laptop?**
Over 12 months Mal buys it for AED 12,000 and adds AED 1,080 profit: a total sale price of AED 13,080, paid as 12 instalments of AED 1,090. Settling after the fifth instalment leaves AED 7,630 owed, less AED 378 ibra' (60% of the AED 630 remaining profit), giving AED 7,252.

## Frequently Asked Questions — Servicing, Problems and Escalation

**What if the item arrives damaged or faulty?**
Report it in the Mal app within the 7 calendar day inspection window. Mal collects at Mal's cost within 3 working days, and a confirmed defect rescinds the Mal Everyday Murabaha sale: the price is cancelled, instalments paid are returned within 5 working days, and Mal keeps no profit. A minor defect can instead be settled by price reduction. Mal, not you, pursues the merchant.

**I cancelled my flight — what happens to my instalments?**
It depends on the carrier refund. A full refund rescinds the Mal Everyday Murabaha contract and everything paid returns within 5 working days. A partial refund reduces the sale price to the retained cost plus the same profit rate on it. A non-refundable fare or no-show leaves the contract standing. Airline-initiated cancellations are always treated as full refunds.

**Can I change my instalment date?**
Yes, once per Mal Everyday Murabaha contract, under My Contracts → Payment date, to any day from the 1st to the 28th with 3 working days' notice. It shifts the calendar only. A realignment forced by an employer changing salary dates does not use up your one change.

**Can I pay off part of my contract early?**
Yes. Partial prepayments on Mal Everyday Murabaha are accepted any time in multiples of AED 100, minimum AED 500, and are applied to the final instalments so the contract ends sooner rather than the monthly amount falling. Ibra' applies only to full settlement.

## Frequently Asked Questions — Products, Agency and Life Events

**What is the difference between Mal Everyday Murabaha and Mal Tawarruq?**
Mal Everyday Murabaha gives you an asset and never disburses cash. Mal Tawarruq is a separate, tightly limited product that gives you cash by selling you a commodity on deferred terms which you then sell on. It is capped at AED 75,000, restricted to Core and Prime tiers, needs a stated purpose, and is declined where Mal Everyday Murabaha could finance the purchase directly.

**Can Mal ask me to buy the item myself?**
Yes, where the supplier is outside Mal's partner network. Mal appoints you as purchasing agent under a wakala; funds go to the supplier, the invoice names Mal as buyer, and you upload invoice and proof of delivery within 7 calendar days. Agency purchases are capped at AED 40,000 and self-dealing is prohibited.

**What happens to my Mal Everyday Murabaha contract if I die?**
It is frozen on notification with a death certificate, with no contact about payment for 90 calendar days. The outstanding sale price becomes a claim on the estate. Mal Takaful cover, where held, settles it directly. Where the estate falls short, Mal writes off balances under AED 25,000 rather than pursue heirs.

**I lost my job — can I pause payments?**
Notify Mal within 30 days. Mal grants a payment holiday of up to 3 months on Mal Everyday Murabaha instalments, with no charity contribution accruing and nothing reported to the credit bureau. The total sale price is unchanged and the schedule extends. Beyond 3 months the case routes to the Mal Late Payment & Charity Policy.

**Do I have to take Takaful cover?**
No. Takaful is offered on Mal Everyday Murabaha contracts of AED 20,000 or more and is never a condition of approval. It costs about 0.35% of asset cost a year, sits outside the sale price, and can be cancelled any time.

**How do I complain, and what if Mal's answer is wrong?**
Raise it under Help → Raise a complaint or email support@mal.example. Mal acknowledges within 1 working day and responds within 5 working days (10 if merchant input is needed). The Mal Customer Review Panel reviews within 10 working days; Sharia questions go to the ISSC at issc@mal.example, which determines within 20 working days. After 30 working days unresolved, escalate to Sanadak, the UAE Central Bank's consumer protection route.

**Which Mal product should I use for a car?**
Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek, not Mal Everyday Murabaha. You lease the vehicle and title transfers at the end, typically over 24 to 60 months, with lower monthly amounts because Mal retains ownership and residual value. Mal Everyday Murabaha still covers accessories, tyres and servicing packages.

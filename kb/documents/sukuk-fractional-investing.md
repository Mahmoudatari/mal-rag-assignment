# Mal Fractional Sukuk — Retail Investment Guidelines

Mal Fractional Sukuk lets a customer own a fractional unit of a Sharia-compliant UAE corporate or sovereign sukuk directly from the Mal app, starting at AED 500 and rising in AED 100 increments. Each unit is real proportionate beneficial ownership of an underlying asset, usufruct or compliant activity, held for the customer by Mal Nominees (ADGM) Limited. Capital invested in Mal Fractional Sukuk is at risk: there is no capital guarantee, distributions are not promised, prices move daily, and in an issuer default a customer can lose the entire amount invested in a listing. Mal Fractional Sukuk is an investment product and is not a deposit.

These guidelines cover what a sukuk is and why it is not a bond; capital risk, default and recovery; custody and nominee mechanics; the Ijara, Murabaha and Wakala structures Mal lists; asset-backed and asset-based listings; Sharia screening, purification and governance; listing-page fields; fees; how orders are placed, batched, settled and cancelled, with worked examples; distributions; the secondary window, maturity, early call and corporate actions; concentration limits, Zakat, statements, inheritance, complaints, a comparison with Mal Digital Wakala savings, a glossary and frequently asked questions.

## What a Sukuk Is, and Why It Is Not a Bond

A conventional bond is a loan: the investor lends, the issuer owes that principal back on a fixed date, and in the meantime pays a coupon calculated as a percentage of the loan. That coupon is riba — a predetermined increase on a debt, earned purely for the passage of time and not for bearing commercial risk. This is why Mal does not offer conventional bonds on any tier of the app, regardless of issuer quality or credit rating.

Mal Fractional Sukuk differs in kind, not merely in wording. A sukuk certificate evidences proportionate beneficial ownership of an identified asset, a usufruct (the right to use an asset), or a defined pool of Sharia-compliant commercial activity. The holder is an owner, not a lender; returns flow from rent, sale profit or agency profit generated by that asset, and the holder is exposed to its performance and its risk.

The test Mal applies to every Mal Fractional Sukuk listing is whether a real asset or activity stands behind the certificate and whether loss can genuinely reach the holder. An instrument that is a sukuk in name but economically a pure obligation to repay principal plus a time-based increase is not listed.

## Capital Risk — Read Before Investing

Capital invested in Mal Fractional Sukuk is at risk. A customer can get back less than they put in, and in a default can lose the entire amount invested in a listing. Mal Fractional Sukuk is an investment product, not a savings product.

There is no capital guarantee of any kind. Neither Mal, Mal Nominees, the custodian, nor any Mal group entity guarantees the return of principal or the payment of any distribution. Sharia compliance is a compliance characteristic, not a safety characteristic — a fully compliant sukuk can and does lose value.

Mal Fractional Sukuk is not a bank deposit and is not covered by any deposit protection or compensation scheme. It does not carry the protections that apply to balances in a Mal current or savings account.

Secondary market prices move daily with profit rate expectations, issuer credit perception and liquidity. Rising market profit rates generally push the price of existing Mal Fractional Sukuk listings down; a customer selling before maturity realises that price, whatever it is on the day.

## Fractional Investing, Custody and Nominee Arrangements

Institutional sukuk trade in large denominations — typically USD 200,000 minimum — which historically excluded retail customers. Mal Fractional Sukuk removes that barrier by acquiring whole certificates and splitting the beneficial interest into fractional units subscribable from AED 500, in AED 100 increments. A Mal Fractional Sukuk unit is real proportionate beneficial ownership, not a synthetic exposure or a derivative referencing a sukuk price.

Orders are placed in-app and executed in aggregated batches at 11:00 and 15:00 Gulf Standard Time on UAE business days. The price at order entry is indicative; the customer receives the achieved batch price, disclosed on the confirmation screen.

Every Mal Fractional Sukuk holding is registered to Mal Nominees (ADGM) Limited, a ring-fenced entity whose sole function is to hold client assets on trust for beneficial owners. Positions sit in an omnibus account with an independent third-party custodian, reconciled to Mal's books daily; cash pending investment and undistributed profit sit in a segregated client money account. If Mal ceased to operate, Mal Fractional Sukuk holdings would not form part of Mal's estate — but that protects against Mal's failure only, not against the sukuk falling in value or the issuer defaulting.

## Sukuk Structures Offered on Mal

Mal Fractional Sukuk currently lists three approved structures. Each listing states its structure on the product screen before the customer confirms an order.

| Structure | Underlying | How profit arises | Typical Mal listings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ijara | Identified tangible asset (property, aircraft, plant) | Lease rentals paid by the lessee for use of the asset | Sovereign and quasi-sovereign |
| Murabaha | Commodity or asset sold on deferred cost-plus terms | Disclosed, fixed mark-up on the sale | Corporate, shorter tenor |
| Wakala | Diversified pool of Sharia-compliant assets and activities | Actual profit of the pool, less the agent's fee | Corporate and financial institution |

**Ijara-based Mal Fractional Sukuk.** Holders own an undivided share in a specific tangible asset leased back to the obligor, and distributions are their proportionate share of the lease rental. Because a real asset is owned, the structure dominates sovereign issuance.

**Murabaha-based Mal Fractional Sukuk.** Proceeds fund an asset sold onward at a disclosed cost-plus-profit price payable in instalments. The mark-up is fixed at contract date and cannot be increased for late payment. Murabaha receivables are debt-like once created, which restricts secondary trading — Mal treats these listings as buy-and-hold and flags them as reduced-liquidity.

**Wakala-based Mal Fractional Sukuk.** Holders appoint the issuer as agent (wakil) to invest proceeds in a defined pool of compliant assets against an expected profit rate. Profit above that rate may be retained by the agent as an incentive fee; profit below it is what the holder receives. The expected profit rate is an expectation, not a promise.

## Asset-Backed Versus Asset-Based Mal Fractional Sukuk

The most consequential field on a Mal Fractional Sukuk listing page is the backing flag, because it decides what a holder owns and what recovery looks like if the obligor fails.

| | Asset-backed | Asset-based |
|---|---|---|
| Legal transfer | True sale of the asset to the issuing vehicle | No true sale; asset stays with the obligor |
| What the holder owns | Proportionate beneficial ownership of the asset | A claim under the obligor's purchase undertaking |
| Recourse on default | To the asset, realised and distributed | To the obligor, ranking as unsecured creditor |
| Recovery expectation | Follows the asset's realisable value | Follows the obligor's general credit; may be nil |
| Typical Mal listings | Sovereign and quasi-sovereign Ijara | Corporate Wakala and Murabaha |

Most sukuk issued globally are asset-based rather than asset-backed, despite the ownership language in their documentation. In an asset-based Mal Fractional Sukuk listing the holder's position on default resembles that of an unsecured creditor of the obligor, even though the certificate is validly a sukuk and the structure is Sharia-compliant.

Mal labels every listing with this flag before the customer confirms an order because the compliance question and the recovery question have different answers: compliance is settled by the Internal Sharia Supervision Committee, recovery by the transfer documents.

## Sharia Screening and Purification

Every issuer and listing on Mal Fractional Sukuk is screened before admission, covering the structure documents, the underlying asset, the obligor's principal business activity and its financial ratios. Issuers whose core business involves conventional lending, alcohol, tobacco, gambling, adult entertainment, or conventional insurance are not admitted.

Where an otherwise compliant obligor derives a small, incidental portion of income from non-compliant sources, Mal calculates a purification ratio annually per listing. The corresponding proportion of the customer's distributions is withheld and donated to charities approved by Mal's Internal Sharia Supervision Committee, and disclosed in the customer's annual statement. Purification is not a fee and is not retained by Mal. If a listing's purification ratio exceeds 5%, Mal delists it and notifies holders.

A customer places an AED 10,000 order into a sovereign Ijara listing on Mal Fractional Sukuk at 10:20 GST, ahead of the 11:00 batch. All figures below are illustrative.

The 0.35% platform fee on AED 10,000 is AED 35.00, charged in addition to order value, so AED 10,035.00 is ring-fenced at placement. ## Periodic Profit Distributions

Mal Fractional Sukuk distributions are paid semi-annually for most listings and quarterly for a small number of corporate issues. The frequency and next scheduled date appear on the product screen and in the app's holdings tab.

Mal Fractional Sukuk distributions are the customer's proportionate share of profit actually generated by the underlying asset — rent received, mark-up realised, or pool profit earned. They are not interest and carry no yield guarantee. Where a listing shows an "expected profit rate", that figure is an indication derived from the issuer's structure documents and may not be met.

Distributions are credited to the customer's Mal cash balance within two UAE business days of Mal Nominees receiving cleared funds from the paying agent. If the obligor fails to pay, no distribution is credited; Mal notifies affected holders in-app within one business day of the missed payment date.

## Secondary Market and Selling Before Maturity

Mal operates an in-app secondary window for eligible Mal Fractional Sukuk listings on UAE business days between 10:00 and 15:00 Gulf Standard Time. Liquidity is best-efforts and is not a commitment to buy: in stressed markets, or where an issuer is under credit review, a listing's window may be suspended. Sell trades settle T+2, with proceeds credited to the customer's Mal cash balance on settlement. Murabaha-structured listings are generally not tradeable in the window and are held to maturity, in line with the restriction on trading debt receivables at other than par.

## Maturity, Redemption, Early Call and Corporate Actions

At scheduled maturity a Mal Fractional Sukuk listing is dissolved: the asset is sold or the purchase undertaking is exercised, and holders receive the dissolution amount — typically par plus accrued unpaid profit. The dissolution amount is not guaranteed and can be below par where the asset has fallen in value or the obligor is distressed. Mal Nominees receives it from the paying agent and Mal credits the customer's cash balance within three UAE business days of cleared receipt. No platform fee and no secondary window fee applies on a maturity redemption.

Some Mal Fractional Sukuk listings carry an issuer call option on defined dates, disclosed on the listing page. Calls tend to be exercised when the issuer can refinance more cheaply, which is when the holder would rather have kept the holding. Mal notifies affected holders in-app within one business day of a call notice and credits proceeds on the same three-business-day basis. Holders have no right to refuse a call.

Because the registered holder is Mal Nominees (ADGM) Limited, corporate action votes are cast by Mal Nominees, not by individual customers. Mal polls beneficial holders on economic and elective actions — tender offers, exchange offers, and anything changing the profit rate, tenor or dissolution amount — with a response deadline at least five UAE business days before the issuer's, then votes the aggregate position in proportion to instructions received. Mal does not poll on administrative matters such as paying agent substitutions, where Mal Nominees follows Internal Sharia Supervision Committee guidance. Where a holder does not respond the default is no action: units are not tendered and the holding continues unchanged.

## Default and Recovery

If an issuer defaults on a Mal Fractional Sukuk listing, distributions stop and the holding may fall sharply in value or to zero. Recovery depends on structure: in an asset-backed Ijara listing, holders have recourse to the underlying asset and recovery follows its realisable value; in an asset-based listing — where the certificate relies on a purchase undertaking from the obligor rather than a true sale — holders rank as unsecured creditors and recovery may be materially lower or nil. Mal states on each listing page which applies.

Mal Nominees participates in restructurings and enforcement on behalf of beneficial holders and passes recovered amounts through proportionately, net of documented recovery costs. Recovery commonly takes years and Mal makes no estimate of outcome.

## Zakat on Mal Fractional Sukuk Holdings

Mal Fractional Sukuk holdings are treated as trade or investment assets for Zakat, so Zakat is calculated on the market value of the holding rather than on the amount originally subscribed. Distributions already credited to the Mal cash balance are added to the Zakatable base.

Zakat becomes payable once total Zakatable wealth exceeds the nisab threshold and has been held for one complete lunar (Hijri) year. Mal publishes the nisab monthly in the app, benchmarked to the prevailing value of 85 grams of gold. The rate is 2.5% of the Zakatable base for a lunar year, or 2.577% where the customer calculates on a Gregorian year instead.

Mal shows an indicative Zakat figure in the holdings tab, computed from Mal Fractional Sukuk market value, Mal Digital Wakala balances and the Mal cash balance on the customer's chosen Zakat anniversary date. Mal does not deduct or pay Zakat on the customer's behalf; timing, payment and choice of recipient remain the customer's responsibility, and customers holding assets outside Mal should calculate on their full wealth.

Zakat on a Mal Fractional Sukuk holding differs from Zakat on a Mal Digital Wakala savings balance in one respect that matters: the savings balance is Zakatable at its nominal value, whereas a sukuk holding is Zakatable at market value, which may be above or below what was subscribed.

## Transferring or Inheriting a Mal Fractional Sukuk Holding

Mal Fractional Sukuk holdings cannot be transferred between living customers. There is no gift, assignment or account-to-account transfer of units; a customer wanting to move value to another person sells in the secondary window and transfers cash.

On notification of a holder's death, Mal freezes the account within one UAE business day, stopping new orders, secondary window sells and withdrawals. Existing Mal Fractional Sukuk holdings are retained and continue to receive distributions into the frozen cash balance, and the custody fee continues to accrue. Mal does not liquidate a deceased customer's holdings on its own initiative, because a forced sale on an arbitrary date can crystallise a loss the estate did not choose.

Mal requires the death certificate, the succession instrument recognised in the relevant UAE jurisdiction, identification for the appointed representative, and where applicable a court order specifying distribution of the estate. Mal Fractional Sukuk positions are then either transferred in specie to heirs who hold or open Mal accounts — preserving units and cost basis, with no platform fee — or liquidated in the secondary window and paid out as cash, at the estate representative's election.

Mal's standard is 10 UAE business days from a complete document set to completion; incomplete submissions are the usual cause of delay, and Mal states what is missing within five working days. Estate queries go to support@mal.example or +971-4-000-0000.

## Common Misconceptions About Mal Fractional Sukuk

**"Sharia-compliant means safe."** It does not. Compliance is a characteristic of the contract, not of the credit. A fully compliant Mal Fractional Sukuk listing can lose value and can default. Safety and compliance are assessed by different people asking different questions.

**"Sukuk are Islamic bonds, so they behave like bonds."** They are not bonds and Mal does not describe them as such. A sukuk holder is an owner exposed to asset performance; a bondholder is a lender owed principal. The two can look similar in a benign market and diverge sharply in a default — exactly when the difference matters.

**"The expected profit rate is guaranteed."** It is an indication drawn from the issuer's structure documents. If the underlying asset underperforms or the obligor does not pay, the customer receives less, or nothing.

**"Asset-backed means my money is protected."** Asset-backed means recourse runs to the asset instead of the obligor's general credit. Recovery still depends on what the asset realises, which can be far below the amount invested, and commonly takes years.

**"Mal picks listings for me."** Mal screens listings for Sharia compliance and admits or rejects them. Mal does not recommend, rank or select listings for a customer, and the Mal AI companion does not give personalised investment advice.

## Glossary and Related Mal Documents

**Sukuk.** Certificates evidencing proportionate beneficial ownership of an asset, usufruct or pool of compliant activity. **Riba.** A predetermined increase on a debt earned for the passage of time rather than for bearing risk — the reason Mal lists no conventional bonds. **Ijara.** A lease; holders own an undivided share in a tangible asset leased to the obligor and receive their share of the rental. **Murabaha.** A sale at disclosed cost plus an agreed mark-up on deferred terms, fixed at contract date. **Wakala.** An agency arrangement in which the issuer invests proceeds as agent (wakil) against an expected profit rate, for a fee.

**Obligor.** The entity whose payment performance the holder depends on — the lessee, buyer or agent behind the listing. **Usufruct.** The right to use and benefit from an asset without owning the asset itself. **Nominee.** An entity holding legal title on trust for beneficial owners; Mal Nominees (ADGM) Limited is the registered holder of every Mal Fractional Sukuk position. **Purification.** Withholding the portion of a distribution attributable to incidental non-compliant income and donating it to approved charity. **Dissolution amount.** The final sum payable per unit when a listing is dissolved at maturity or on a call. **Nisab.** The wealth threshold above which Zakat is payable, benchmarked by Mal to 85 grams of gold. **Asset-backed and asset-based.** True sale with recourse to the asset, versus no true sale with recourse to the obligor under a purchase undertaking.

Related Mal documents: **Mal Digital Wakala Savings** (the capital-preservation savings product, agency fee 0.45%); **Mal Everyday Murabaha** (retail cost-plus financing); **Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek** (lease-to-own financing); and the **Mal Late Payment and Charity Policy** (how late payment amounts are treated and donated).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Mal Fractional Sukuk capital guaranteed?**

No. Mal Fractional Sukuk carries no capital guarantee whatsoever. Capital is at risk, market prices move daily, and in an issuer default a customer can lose the full amount invested in that listing. Mal Fractional Sukuk is not a deposit and carries no deposit protection or compensation scheme cover.

**What is the minimum amount I can invest in Mal Fractional Sukuk?**

The minimum ticket is AED 500 per listing, with additional increments of AED 100. There is no minimum account balance and no account opening fee.

**Why can Mal offer sukuk but not conventional bonds?**

A conventional bond is a loan repaid with a predetermined time-based increase, which is riba, so Mal does not offer bonds. A Mal Fractional Sukuk certificate is proportionate beneficial ownership of an asset, usufruct or compliant activity, returning a share of the profit that asset actually generates.

**Are the distributions on Mal Fractional Sukuk fixed?**

No. Distributions are the customer's share of profit actually generated by the underlying asset and are not guaranteed interest. Listings display an expected profit rate as an indication only. If the obligor does not pay, no distribution is credited, and Mal notifies holders in-app within one business day.

**Can I sell my Mal Fractional Sukuk holding before maturity?**

Eligible listings can be sold in Mal's in-app secondary window on UAE business days between 10:00 and 15:00 Gulf Standard Time, settling T+2. Liquidity is best-efforts and may be suspended in stressed markets. Murabaha-structured listings are generally held to maturity and are flagged as reduced-liquidity in the app.

**What happens to my Mal Fractional Sukuk if the issuer defaults?**

Distributions stop and the holding may fall sharply in value or to zero. In an asset-backed Ijara listing, holders have recourse to the underlying asset; in an asset-based listing, holders rank as unsecured creditors of the obligor and recovery may be nil. Mal Nominees pursues recovery on holders' behalf and passes recovered amounts through proportionately, but Mal Fractional Sukuk units are registered to Mal Nominees and segregated from Mal's balance sheet, which protects against Mal's failure only — not against issuer default.

**Does Sharia-compliant mean my Mal Fractional Sukuk investment is safe?**

No. Compliance describes the contract, not the credit. A fully compliant Mal Fractional Sukuk listing can fall in value and can default, and the customer can lose the entire amount invested in it.

**Do I pay Zakat on a Mal Fractional Sukuk holding?**

Zakat is 2.5% of the market value of the holding for a lunar year, once total Zakatable wealth exceeds the nisab. Mal publishes the nisab monthly and shows an indicative figure in the app, but does not deduct or pay Zakat automatically.

**What happens to my Mal Fractional Sukuk holding at maturity?**

The listing is dissolved and holders receive the dissolution amount, typically par plus accrued unpaid profit but potentially less. Mal credits proceeds within three UAE business days of cleared receipt, with no platform fee or window fee on a maturity redemption.

**Can the issuer end a Mal Fractional Sukuk listing early?**

Yes, where the listing carries an issuer call option, disclosed under tenor on the listing page. Mal notifies holders in-app within one business day of a call notice and credits proceeds on the same three-business-day basis. Holders cannot refuse a call.

**Can I cancel a Mal Fractional Sukuk order?**

Yes, free of charge, while the order is Placed or Queued — in practice up to 15 minutes before the 11:00 or 15:00 GST batch cut-off. After execution the order is irrevocable and there is no cooling-off period.

**Should I choose Mal Fractional Sukuk or Mal Digital Wakala savings?**

Mal Digital Wakala savings is the capital-preservation option; Mal Fractional Sukuk is not. Sukuk holdings move in market value daily and are exposed to issuer default, while a Wakala balance is not exposed to market pricing. Neither guarantees a return, and Mal does not advise which to choose.

**What happens to my Mal Fractional Sukuk holding if I die?**

Mal freezes the account within one UAE business day and retains rather than liquidates the holdings. On receipt of the death certificate, recognised succession instrument and representative identification, positions are transferred in specie to heirs with Mal accounts or liquidated for cash, within 10 UAE business days of a complete document set.

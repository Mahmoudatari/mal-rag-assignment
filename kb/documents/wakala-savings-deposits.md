# Mal Digital Wakala — Savings & Deposit Account Terms

Mal Digital Wakala is the savings and deposit product family offered through the Mal app, under which the customer appoints Mal as an investment agent (*wakil*) to place their funds in the Mal Sharia-Compliant Investment Pool. It covers the open-ended Wakala Savings tiers and the fixed-term Wakala deposits, and it sits alongside the *Qard*-based Mal Everyday Account, which is guaranteed but pays no profit.

This document is the complete terms set for Mal Digital Wakala. It covers the agency contract and why it is not interest; the composition of the investment pool; anticipated versus actual profit rates; Mal's fixed agency fee and incentive share; worked profit and early-break arithmetic; loss handling and Mal's negligence exception; the account tiers and their thresholds; profit accrual and crediting; account opening, KYC, joint and minors' accounts; deposits, transfer limits and cut-off times; statements and profit history; Zakat; dormancy, closure, death and succession; purification of non-permissible income; security and fraud; complaints and escalation; Sharia governance; a glossary; and a set of frequently asked questions. All figures, rates, timelines and contact details in this document are illustrative and synthetic.

## The Wakala Contract

Mal Digital Wakala is structured as a *wakala bil istithmar* — an agency for investment. The customer is the principal (*muwakkil*) and the owner of the funds at all times. Mal acts solely as the investment agent (*wakil*), authorised to place those funds into the Mal Sharia-Compliant Investment Pool on the customer's behalf.

The agency is created when the customer accepts the Mal Digital Wakala terms in the app and funds the account. Acceptance is recorded digitally with a timestamp, the accepted tenor, and the anticipated profit rate shown on screen at that moment.

Mal's authority under Mal Digital Wakala is limited. Funds may only be deployed into asset classes approved by Mal's Internal Sharia Supervision Committee — Murabaha trade receivables, Ijara lease portfolios, Sukuk holdings and short-term Sharia-compliant interbank Wakala placements — never into conventional interest-bearing instruments, and never pledged or lent outside the approved pool.

## Why Mal Digital Wakala Is Not Interest

Under Mal Digital Wakala the customer does not lend money to Mal. Mal never becomes a borrower of the balance and no fixed sum is contractually owed back; what the customer receives is a share of profit actually generated by investing their funds in the Mal Sharia-Compliant Investment Pool.

A conventional deposit pays a rate on a debt regardless of what the bank earns; that is *riba*. Mal Digital Wakala reverses the structure: the return depends entirely on realised investment performance. For the same reason Mal cannot guarantee the principal of a Mal Digital Wakala savings or fixed-term deposit — a guarantee of principal by the agent would convert the agency into a loan and invalidate the contract. The only exception is Mal's own negligence or breach, dealt with below.

## Anticipated Profit Rate vs Actual Profit Rate

Every Mal Digital Wakala account displays an **anticipated profit rate** — for example 4.10% per annum on the Wakala Savings tier. The anticipated rate is Mal's good-faith projection of what the Mal Sharia-Compliant Investment Pool is expected to generate, based on the current asset mix and historical performance.

The anticipated profit rate is an indication, not a promise. The customer's return is the **actual profit rate** — the profit genuinely realised by the pool over the period, net of the agency fee, attributed to the customer's balance. The actual rate may be lower than the anticipated rate, and in a poor period may be zero.

Mal publishes the actual rate for each completed period in the app under Savings → Profit History. Over the last eight quarters, actual rates on Wakala Savings ranged between 3.62% and 4.28% p.a. against anticipated rates of 3.90% to 4.10% p.a.

## Mal's Agency Fee and Incentive Share

As *wakil*, Mal is entitled to a fee for managing the Mal Digital Wakala pool. The fee has two components, both disclosed in the app before funding. The first is a **fixed agency fee** of 0.45% per annum of the average daily balance, accrued daily and deducted from gross pool profit before the customer's share is calculated. It is charged whether or not the pool meets its anticipated rate — a fee for the service of agency, not a share of profit — and is capped at AED 2,500 per customer per profit period.

The second is an **incentive fee**. Where actual profit on a Mal Digital Wakala account exceeds the anticipated profit rate for that period, the excess above the anticipated rate is retained by Mal as *wakil* as a performance incentive. In practice the customer receives up to the anticipated rate, and Mal keeps the surplus above it. Mal charges no account opening fee, no monthly maintenance fee and no minimum-balance penalty on Mal Digital Wakala accounts.

## Loss Handling

Losses in the Mal Sharia-Compliant Investment Pool are borne by the customer as principal (*muwakkil*), in proportion to their share of the pool, and reduce the Mal Digital Wakala balance. The funds remain the customer's property, and the owner of capital bears the risk attaching to it.

There is one exception, and it is precise. Mal bears the loss where the loss is caused by Mal's **negligence, wilful misconduct, or breach of the Mal Digital Wakala terms** — including deployment of funds outside the approved asset classes, or acting beyond the authority granted in the agency. In those circumstances Mal indemnifies the customer for the loss attributable to that conduct, and the fixed agency fee for the affected period is refunded.

Outside that exception, Mal does not compensate for market losses. A customer who believes a loss falls within it may claim in the app under Support → Wakala Claim or at wakala-claims@mal.example; claims are assessed within 20 business days.

## Account Tiers

Mal offers three structures. The Mal Everyday Account is a current account structured as *Qard* (a loan from customer to Mal): guaranteed in full, repayable on demand, and precisely because it is guaranteed it earns no profit. Mal Digital Wakala Savings is open-ended with instant access; Mal Digital Wakala Fixed-Term commits funds for a set tenor at a higher anticipated rate.

| Tier | Structure | Minimum balance | Tenor | Anticipated rate p.a. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mal Everyday Account | Qard (current) | AED 0 | On demand | 0.00% |
| Wakala Savings — Core | Wakala | AED 1,000 | Open-ended | 3.25% |
| Wakala Savings — Plus | Wakala | AED 25,000 | Open-ended | 4.10% |
| Wakala Fixed-Term 3M | Wakala | AED 10,000 | 3 months | 4.35% |
| Wakala Fixed-Term 6M | Wakala | AED 10,000 | 6 months | 4.60% |
| Wakala Fixed-Term 12M | Wakala | AED 50,000 | 12 months | 5.05% |

Tier upgrades on Mal Digital Wakala Savings are automatic: if the average daily balance crosses AED 25,000 for a full calendar month, the account moves to the Plus anticipated rate from the following month, and downgrades apply on the same basis. A customer may hold one Mal Everyday Account, one Mal Digital Wakala Savings account, and up to ten concurrent Fixed-Term deposits.

## Profit Calculation, Accrual and Crediting

Profit on Mal Digital Wakala accounts is calculated on the **average daily balance** over the profit period, actual/365. Funds received before 14:00 Gulf Standard Time accrue from the same day, later funds from the next day.

Profit accrues daily but is realised and credited only at period end. For Mal Digital Wakala Savings the period is **monthly**, credited on the first business day of the following month. For Mal Digital Wakala Fixed-Term deposits of six months or longer it is **quarterly**, credited the first business day after quarter end; three-month deposits pay once at maturity.

Accrued but uncredited profit is shown in the Mal app as an indicative figure labelled "accruing — not yet realised"; it is not part of the withdrawable balance and may change before crediting. Withdrawals from Mal Digital Wakala Savings during a profit period do not forfeit accrued profit, but they reduce the average daily balance and therefore the profit earned. A mid-month withdrawal that pulls the average daily balance below the Plus threshold will also cause that month to be settled at the Core anticipated rate rather than the Plus rate.

## Early Withdrawal and Breaking a Fixed-Term Wakala

Mal Digital Wakala Fixed-Term deposits may be broken at any time from the app, with no paperwork and no branch visit. Breaking is irreversible and immediate. A Mal Digital Wakala Fixed-Term deposit broken before maturity is settled at the anticipated rate of the **next-shortest completed tenor**, less an early-termination adjustment of 0.50% per annum applied over the period actually held. A 12-month deposit broken at month seven is therefore settled at the 6-month anticipated rate of 4.60% less 0.50%, giving 4.10% p.a. over seven months. A deposit broken before completing three months earns the Wakala Savings Core anticipated rate of 3.25% p.a., less the same adjustment.

Principal plus recalculated profit returns to the customer's Mal Everyday Account within one business day. Mal charges no separate breakage fee beyond that rate adjustment.

## Data, Balances and Account Numbers in the App

Mal holds the customer's identity data (name, Emirates ID, nationality, date of birth), contact details, source-of-funds declaration, Mal Digital Wakala balances, transaction history and profit history, retained for the life of the relationship and five years afterwards. In the Mal app, Mal Digital Wakala account numbers are masked by default and displayed in the form `MAL-****-****-4417`, showing only the last four digits. The full account number and IBAN are revealed only after in-app biometric re-authentication, and are never included in push notifications, SMS, or email from Mal.

Balances display in AED to two decimals and refresh in real time, and Privacy Mode hides them from the home screen. Mal support agents see masked account numbers only and will never ask a customer to disclose a full account number, Emirates ID number, or one-time passcode.

## Sharia Governance and Deposit Protection

The Mal Digital Wakala product set, its contract templates, its profit-distribution methodology and its agency and incentive fee structure are approved by Mal's Internal Sharia Supervision Committee (ISSC), five independent scholars who review the Mal Sharia-Compliant Investment Pool quarterly and publish an annual compliance report in the Mal app.

Mal Digital Wakala aligns with the AAOIFI Sharia Standards on agency (Wakala) and investment-agency profit distribution. Where the ISSC later finds a transaction non-compliant, income attributable to it is quarantined and donated to charity through Mal's purification account rather than distributed to customers.

Mal Digital Wakala savings and fixed-term balances are **investment accounts, not guaranteed deposits**, and are not covered by any conventional deposit-insurance or deposit-guarantee scheme. Mal Everyday Account balances, being *Qard*-based, are guaranteed by Mal in full. This distinction is shown on every Mal Digital Wakala funding screen.

## What the Mal Sharia-Compliant Investment Pool Holds

Funds placed under Mal Digital Wakala are commingled into a single Mal Sharia-Compliant Investment Pool. The pool is managed to a target asset allocation approved annually by Mal's Internal Sharia Supervision Committee, with permitted ranges that allow the treasury desk to respond to liquidity conditions without re-approval.

| Asset class | Target | Permitted range | Average tenor | Credit quality |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Murabaha trade receivables | 40% | 30–50% | 5.5 months | Internal grades 1–4 |
| Ijara lease portfolios | 25% | 15–35% | 38 months | Internal grades 1–3 |

The blended average tenor of the Mal Sharia-Compliant Investment Pool is approximately 14 months, which is what allows Mal Digital Wakala Savings to offer instant access while the underlying assets run longer. No single obligor may exceed 5% of the pool and no single sector 25%. Conventional bonds, interest-bearing money-market instruments, speculative derivatives and any exposure to alcohol, tobacco, gambling, conventional insurance, adult entertainment or conventional banking are prohibited outright. Quarter-end pool composition is published under Savings → Pool Composition.

## Worked Example: A Month of Profit on Wakala Savings

The following illustrates a full month of profit on a Mal Digital Wakala Savings — Plus account. Assume the customer holds AED 40,000 for the whole of a 30-day month, so the average daily balance is AED 40,000, and the Plus anticipated profit rate is 4.10% p.a.

Step one — gross pool profit attributed to the balance. Assume the Mal Sharia-Compliant Investment Pool returned a gross 4.85% p.a. for the month. Gross profit = 40,000 × 4.85% × 30 / 365 = **AED 159.45**.

Step two — Mal's fixed agency fee of 0.45% p.a. on the average daily balance. Fee = 40,000 × 0.45% × 30 / 365 = **AED 14.79**. This is far below the AED 2,500 per-period cap, so the cap does not bite.

Step three — net profit before the incentive fee = 159.45 − 14.79 = **AED 144.66**, equivalent to 4.40% p.a.

Step four — the incentive fee. The customer receives up to the anticipated rate of 4.10% p.a., which is 40,000 × 4.10% × 30 / 365 = **AED 134.79**. The excess of 144.66 − 134.79 = **AED 9.87** is retained by Mal as *wakil*.

The customer is credited **AED 134.79** on the first business day of the following month, and the app records an actual profit rate of 4.10% p.a. Had the pool returned a gross 3.90% p.a. instead, net profit after the agency fee would have been AED 113.43 — about 3.45% p.a., below the anticipated rate, with no incentive fee arising.

## Worked Example: Breaking a Fixed-Term Deposit

A customer places AED 100,000 into a Mal Digital Wakala Fixed-Term 12M deposit on 1 March at an anticipated rate of 5.05% p.a., then breaks it in the Mal app on 1 October — seven months, or 214 actual days, into the tenor.

Held to maturity, the deposit would have anticipated 100,000 × 5.05% × 365 / 365 = **AED 5,050.00** of profit.

On breaking, the settlement rate is the anticipated rate of the next-shortest completed tenor. Seven months completes the 6-month tenor but not the 12-month, so the 6M anticipated rate of 4.60% p.a. applies, less the early-termination adjustment of 0.50% p.a., giving **4.10% p.a.** over the period actually held.

Recalculated profit = 100,000 × 4.10% × 214 / 365 = **AED 2,403.29**.

Quarterly profit already credited on the deposit during the tenor is netted off. If AED 1,700.00 had already been credited at the two completed quarter ends at the 5.05% anticipated rate, the over-credited amount is recovered from the settlement, so the customer receives principal of AED 100,000.00 plus 2,403.29 − 1,700.00 = **AED 703.29**, a total of **AED 100,703.29**, returned to the Mal Everyday Account within one business day. Mal charges no separate breakage fee. Had the same deposit been broken at month two, before completing any three-month tenor, it would have been settled at the Wakala Savings Core anticipated rate of 3.25% less 0.50%, that is 2.75% p.a.

## Why Mal Chose Wakala over Mudaraba

Three Sharia structures could carry a savings product. Mal Digital Wakala uses *wakala bil istithmar*; the alternatives were *mudaraba* and *qard*.

| Structure | Bank's role | Bank's remuneration | Principal guaranteed | Who directs investment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wakala | Agent (*wakil*) | Fixed agency fee + incentive above anticipated rate | No | Customer, via a mandate to the agent |
| Mudaraba | Entrepreneur (*mudarib*) | Pre-agreed share of profit, e.g. 30/70 | No | Bank, at its own discretion |
| Qard | Borrower | None — retains all return | Yes, in full | Bank, as owner of the borrowed funds |

Mal chose Wakala for three reasons. First, pricing transparency: a fixed 0.45% p.a. agency fee is a number the customer can check against their own balance, whereas a Mudaraba profit-share ratio only becomes a figure after the fact. Second, mandate control: the customer remains principal and the mandate is contractually bounded to approved asset classes, so Mal's discretion is narrower than a *mudarib*'s. Third, symmetry: fixed-term Wakala placements are the standard Islamic interbank instrument, so Mal's retail liabilities and treasury placements share one structure.

*Qard* was retained for the Mal Everyday Account precisely because a current account needs a guarantee — and, being a loan, it can pay no return at all without becoming *riba*.

## Opening an Account: Eligibility and KYC

A Mal Digital Wakala account may be opened by any individual aged 21 or over who holds a valid Emirates ID in the format `784-YYYY-NNNNNNN-N` and a UAE mobile number. Applicants aged 18 to 20 may open a Mal Digital Wakala Savings — Core account but not a Fixed-Term deposit. Under-18s are served only through the guardian-operated route described elsewhere in these terms.

Onboarding is entirely in-app. The customer scans the Emirates ID, completes a liveness check, and Mal verifies the identity against the UAE Pass federated identity service. Median verification time is under four minutes; cases routed to manual review are resolved within one business day.

A source-of-funds declaration is mandatory at opening. Documentary evidence — salary certificate, sale contract, audited accounts or an inheritance deed — is required where a single credit exceeds AED 150,000, or where cumulative credits exceed AED 500,000 in any rolling 12 months. Mal may request evidence below those thresholds where the profile warrants it.

Non-residents may open a Mal Digital Wakala Savings account with a passport, a valid UAE entry record and proof of overseas address, subject to enhanced due diligence taking up to five business days; they are not eligible for the 12-month Fixed-Term tier. Politically exposed persons require senior-management approval, adding up to seven business days.

## Joint Accounts and Accounts for Minors

A Mal Digital Wakala Savings account may be held jointly by up to four individuals, each of whom must independently complete Mal's KYC. All joint holders are principals (*muwakkil*) together and each must accept the agency in their own app session; the account is not funded until every holder has accepted. Joint holdings are "either to operate" by default, meaning any single holder may deposit, withdraw or break a Fixed-Term deposit; holders may switch the mandate to "all to operate" in-app, which then requires every holder to approve each withdrawal within a 24-hour window.

Profit on a joint Mal Digital Wakala account is attributed to the account, not to individual holders, and is credited in the shares recorded at opening — equal shares unless the holders specify otherwise. Tier thresholds, including the AED 25,000 Plus threshold, are assessed on the whole account balance.

For minors under 21, a guardian may open a Mal Digital Wakala Savings — Core account in the minor's name under a guardianship mandate. The guardian accepts the agency on the minor's behalf, withdrawals may be paid only to an account in the minor's name or to the guardian's own Mal Everyday Account, and Fixed-Term tenors may not run past the minor's 21st birthday, when the mandate lapses automatically and the holder must re-accept the Mal Digital Wakala terms within 60 days.

## Zakat on Mal Digital Wakala Balances

Zakat is an annual obligation of 2.5% on qualifying wealth held for one lunar (hijri) year, payable once the holding reaches *nisab* — the minimum threshold, conventionally the value of 85 grams of gold or 595 grams of silver. Mal Digital Wakala balances are cash-equivalent investment assets and are generally Zakatable in full, principal and credited profit alike, because the customer remains the owner of the funds throughout.

Mal calculates an **indicative Zakat figure** for each customer in the Mal app under More → Zakat. The calculation takes the lowest balance held across the hijri year on each Mal Digital Wakala account, applies the customer's chosen *nisab* basis (gold or silver, with the silver basis producing a lower threshold and therefore a wider obligation), and applies 2.5%. The app shows the hijri anniversary date Mal has recorded, which the customer may edit.

Mal does **not** deduct Zakat automatically from any Mal Digital Wakala account. The figure is indicative only, does not account for wealth held outside Mal or deductible debts, and is not a fatwa. Customers who wish to pay through Mal may do so from the Zakat screen to any vetted charitable channel listed there; payment executes within one business day with an in-app receipt. Complex circumstances warrant the customer's own scholar, or sharia@mal.example.

## Deposits, Transfers, Limits and Cut-Off Times

A Mal Digital Wakala account is funded from the customer's Mal Everyday Account, by local transfer over the UAE Funds Transfer System, by international transfer to Mal's IBAN, or by debit-card top-up. Mal Digital Wakala accounts cannot be funded in cash and do not accept cheques.

Funds credited before **14:00 Gulf Standard Time** on a business day accrue profit from that same day; funds credited after the cut-off, or on a weekend or UAE public holiday, accrue from the next business day. International transfers are value-dated on the day Mal receives cleared funds, not the day the sender instructs.

Third-party transfers out of a Mal Digital Wakala account must route through the Mal Everyday Account first. ## Dormancy, Unclaimed Balances and Account Closure

A Mal Digital Wakala account is classified **inactive** after 12 consecutive months with no customer-initiated transaction, and **dormant** after 36 consecutive months. Profit crediting is not a customer-initiated transaction and does not reset the clock; a login alone does not either. Mal notifies the customer at 9, 11, 24 and 35 months by in-app message, SMS and email.

A dormant Mal Digital Wakala account continues to accrue and receive profit at the applicable tier rate, but outbound transfers are blocked. Reactivation is done in-app by completing a biometric re-authentication and confirming current KYC details, and takes effect within one business day; where the Emirates ID has expired, a re-scan is required first.

Where an account remains dormant and the customer cannot be traced, the balance is treated as unclaimed and transferred to the UAE Central Bank's unclaimed balances arrangements after five years of dormancy, in line with the applicable UAE Central Bank regulations. Mal retains the customer's records and the customer's claim survives the transfer; claims are then made through Mal, which files them with the Central Bank within ten business days.

A customer may close a Mal Digital Wakala account at any time in-app, with no closure fee. Closing a Savings account crystallises accrued but uncredited profit at the pool's actual rate for the part-period, paid with the balance to the Mal Everyday Account within one business day. Closing a Fixed-Term deposit early is a break, settled on the early-withdrawal basis set out in these terms.

## Death of an Account Holder and Succession

On receiving credible notice of the death of a Mal Digital Wakala account holder, Mal freezes the account immediately. Freezing stops all outbound transfers, standing instructions and card activity. Profit continues to accrue and to be credited to a frozen Mal Digital Wakala account at the applicable tier rate until distribution, because the funds remain the estate's property and continue to be invested in the Mal Sharia-Compliant Investment Pool. Accrued but uncredited profit at the date of death is crystallised and forms part of the estate.

Fixed-Term deposits are **not** broken automatically on death. The estate's representative may either leave the deposit to run to maturity or request an early break, which is then settled on the ordinary early-withdrawal basis with the 0.50% p.a. adjustment.

To release funds Mal requires: the death certificate (attested and legalised if issued outside the UAE), a succession certificate or court distribution order from a UAE court or an order recognised in the UAE, the Emirates ID or passport of each heir or of the appointed administrator, and Mal's estate release form signed by the administrator. Mal distributes strictly in the shares stated in that order, and does not itself determine shares.

Where the deceased held a registered will with the Abu Dhabi Judicial Department or the DIFC Wills Service, distribution follows that will as admitted by the relevant court. Mal completes distribution within ten business days of a complete document set, through a dedicated estates team at support@mal.example.

## Purification of Non-Permissible Income

Despite screening, small amounts of non-permissible income can enter the Mal Sharia-Compliant Investment Pool — a late-payment amount received on a Murabaha receivable, an incidental interest credit on a nostro account held with a conventional correspondent bank, or income from a Sukuk issuer whose activities drift out of compliance between reviews.

Mal identifies such amounts through a monthly screening run by the Sharia compliance function, which tests every income line in the pool against the approved-income taxonomy and flags exceptions. Flagged income is quarantined at source into the Mal Purification Account and is excluded from the pool's gross profit before the agency fee and any customer attribution is calculated. It therefore never reaches a customer's Mal Digital Wakala balance, is never counted in the actual profit rate, and is never recognised as revenue by Mal.

Quarantined amounts are disbursed quarterly to charitable causes vetted by Mal's Internal Sharia Supervision Committee, with no tax, publicity or reputational benefit taken by Mal. The ISSC signs off every disbursement and the annual total is disclosed in its compliance report in the Mal app. Charity arising from late payment on Mal's financing products is governed by the separate Mal Late Payment & Charity document.

## Security, Fraud and Authentication

Access to a Mal Digital Wakala account requires the customer's device passcode plus biometrics. Revealing a full Mal account number in the form `MAL-nnnn-nnnn-nnnn`, revealing the IBAN, generating a certified statement, adding a new transfer beneficiary, or raising a transfer limit each require a fresh biometric re-authentication, and beneficiaries added in the last 12 hours are capped at AED 5,000 in total.

Every outbound transfer is signed in-app: the customer approves a signing prompt showing the beneficiary, amount and last four digits of the destination account. Mal does not use SMS one-time passcodes for transfer approval, precisely because they are interceptable and socially engineerable.

Mal will **never** ask a customer — by phone, SMS, email, WhatsApp or in-app chat — for a full account number, a full Emirates ID number in the format `784-YYYY-NNNNNNN-N`, a password, a passcode, a one-time code, or biometric data, and will never ask a customer to move funds to a "safe account" or to install remote-access software. Mal support agents see account numbers masked as `MAL-****-****-4417` and cannot see credentials at all.

Report suspected fraud in-app under Support → Report Fraud or on +971-4-000-0000, staffed 24/7; Mal freezes the account within two minutes. Where an unauthorised transaction occurs without the customer's negligence and is reported within 30 days of the statement showing it, Mal refunds within ten business days of concluding its investigation. Where the customer disclosed credentials or approved the signing prompt, liability rests with the customer.

## Complaints, Disputes and Escalation

A complaint about a Mal Digital Wakala account is raised in the Mal app under Support → Complaints, by email to support@mal.example, or by phone on +971-4-000-0000. Every complaint receives a reference number immediately and a named handler.

Mal acknowledges within two working days and issues a substantive response within ten working days. Complaints requiring a profit recalculation, or falling under the Wakala claims route at wakala-claims@mal.example, are assessed within 20 business days; where more time is needed the customer is told why and given a revised date, and the total never exceeds 30 working days.

If the customer is unsatisfied with the response, the complaint escalates internally to Mal's Customer Advocacy Office, which reviews independently of the original handler and responds within a further ten working days.

Complaints turning on Sharia treatment — the characterisation of the incentive fee, whether an asset was permissibly held in the Mal Sharia-Compliant Investment Pool, whether purification was correctly applied — are referred to Mal's Internal Sharia Supervision Committee, which considers them quarterly or, if urgent, by written resolution within 15 working days. Its determination on the Sharia question binds Mal. Request a referral at sharia@mal.example.

Where the customer remains unsatisfied after Mal's final response, or where 30 working days have passed without one, the customer may take the complaint to the UAE Central Bank's consumer protection route, Sanadak, as the final external step. Mal cooperates fully and provides the complete case file.

## Glossary of Terms Used in These Terms

**Wakala bil istithmar** — an agency for investment: the contract underlying Mal Digital Wakala, in which one party appoints another to invest funds on its behalf for a fee.

**Muwakkil** — the principal in a Wakala, the party whose funds are invested. In Mal Digital Wakala this is always the customer, who owns the funds throughout.

**Wakil** — the agent in a Wakala, appointed to act within a defined mandate for a fee. This is Mal, which invests but never owns or borrows the customer's funds.

**Qard** — an interest-free loan. The Mal Everyday Account is a *Qard* from the customer to Mal: guaranteed in full and therefore, necessarily, paying no return.

**Mudaraba** — a partnership in which one party supplies capital and the other supplies management, sharing profit in a pre-agreed ratio while capital loss falls on the capital provider. Not used by Mal Digital Wakala.

**Riba** — any contractually stipulated increase on a loan; prohibited. A conventional deposit paying a rate on money lent to a bank is *riba*, which is why Mal Digital Wakala is structured as an agency rather than a deposit.

**Nisab** — the minimum threshold of wealth above which Zakat becomes payable, conventionally the value of 85 grams of gold or 595 grams of silver.

**Purification** — identifying income that is not Sharia-compliant, quarantining it, and disbursing it to charity rather than distributing or recognising it.

**Anticipated profit rate** — Mal's good-faith projection of what the Mal Sharia-Compliant Investment Pool will generate, shown before funding; an indication, not a promise.

**Actual profit rate** — the profit genuinely realised over a completed period, net of the fixed agency fee, and the figure the customer is actually credited on.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is my money safe in a Mal Digital Wakala savings account?**
Mal Digital Wakala savings balances are invested, not guaranteed, because a guarantee by the agent would turn the Wakala into an interest-bearing loan. Mal indemnifies the customer for losses caused by Mal's own negligence, misconduct or breach of the Wakala terms, but ordinary investment losses are borne by the customer as principal.

**Will I definitely receive the 4.10% anticipated rate?**
No. On Mal Digital Wakala accounts, 4.10% is the anticipated profit rate — an indication of expected performance, not a commitment. The customer receives the actual profit realised by the Mal Sharia-Compliant Investment Pool, net of the agency fee, which may be lower than the anticipated rate. Actual rates for every completed period are published in the app under Savings → Profit History.

**What happens if the pool earns more than the anticipated rate?**
Any profit above the anticipated rate on a Mal Digital Wakala account is retained by Mal as *wakil* as an incentive fee. The customer receives up to the anticipated rate; the surplus is Mal's performance reward for managing the agency. This is separate from and additional to the fixed agency fee of 0.45% per annum.

**What do I lose if I break a 12-month Mal Digital Wakala Fixed-Term deposit early?**
The deposit is re-settled at the anticipated rate of the next-shortest completed tenor, less an early-termination adjustment of 0.50% per annum over the period actually held. Broken at month seven, a 12-month deposit is settled at 4.60% less 0.50% — that is 4.10% p.a. — instead of the 5.05% it would have earned at maturity. There is no additional breakage fee, and funds return to the Mal Everyday Account within one business day.

**Does Mal charge me anything if the investment makes no profit?**
Yes. The fixed agency fee of 0.45% per annum is a fee for the service of acting as agent and is charged regardless of pool performance, subject to the AED 2,500 per-period cap. The incentive fee, by contrast, only arises where actual profit exceeds the anticipated rate. If a loss arises from Mal's negligence or breach of the Mal Digital Wakala terms, the fixed agency fee for that period is refunded.

**Who do I contact about a Mal Digital Wakala profit calculation I disagree with?**
Raise a query in the Mal app under Support → Wakala Claim, email wakala-claims@mal.example, or call +971-4-000-0000. Mal responds within 20 business days, and disputes involving Sharia treatment escalate to the Internal Sharia Supervision Committee.

**Do I pay Zakat on my Mal Digital Wakala balance, and does Mal deduct it?**
Mal Digital Wakala balances are generally Zakatable in full — principal and credited profit — at 2.5% once held above *nisab* for a full lunar year. Mal shows an indicative figure under More → Zakat, using the lowest balance across the hijri year and the customer's chosen gold or silver *nisab* basis, but never deducts Zakat automatically. Payment is the customer's own act, made from that screen to the vetted charitable channels listed there.

**Can I open a Mal Digital Wakala account jointly with my spouse?**
Yes — up to four individuals. Each completes Mal's KYC separately and must accept the agency in their own app session before the account is funded. The default mandate is "either to operate"; holders can switch to "all to operate", requiring every holder to approve each withdrawal within 24 hours. Tier thresholds such as the AED 25,000 Plus threshold are assessed on the whole account balance.

**What happens to my Mal Digital Wakala money if I die?**
Mal freezes the account on credible notice of death, but the funds stay invested in the Mal Sharia-Compliant Investment Pool and keep earning profit until distribution. Accrued but uncredited profit at the date of death is crystallised into the estate, and Fixed-Term deposits are not broken automatically. Mal releases funds against a death certificate, a succession certificate or court order, heir identification and Mal's estate release form, distributing in the shares that order states within ten business days of a complete document set.

**What happens if my Mal Digital Wakala account goes dormant?**
It is inactive after 12 months without a customer-initiated transaction and dormant after 36; profit crediting and logins do not reset that clock. A dormant Mal Digital Wakala account still receives profit, but outbound transfers are blocked. Reactivate in-app with biometric re-authentication and a KYC confirmation, effective within one business day. After five years of dormancy with no contact, the balance passes to the UAE Central Bank's unclaimed balances arrangements, and the claim survives that transfer.

**Is my money lent to Mal, or does Mal use it as its own?**
Neither. The customer remains owner of the funds and Mal acts only as investment agent, placing them in the Mal Sharia-Compliant Investment Pool within a bounded mandate. Mal never becomes a borrower and owes no fixed sum back — which is exactly why the balance is not guaranteed, since a guarantee by the agent would convert the agency into a loan. Only the *Qard*-based Mal Everyday Account is a loan to Mal, and being a loan it pays nothing.

**What is actually in the investment pool?**
The Mal Sharia-Compliant Investment Pool targets 40% Murabaha trade receivables, 25% Ijara lease portfolios, 20% Sukuk, 12% short-term interbank Wakala placements and 3% cash, each within a permitted range set annually by the Internal Sharia Supervision Committee. Blended average tenor is about 14 months, no single obligor may exceed 5% of the pool and no sector 25%, and conventional bonds and prohibited sectors are excluded outright. Quarter-end composition is published under Savings → Pool Composition.

**How do I complain, and what if Mal's answer does not satisfy me?**
Raise it in the Mal app under Support → Complaints, at support@mal.example, or on +971-4-000-0000. Mal acknowledges within two working days and responds substantively within ten; profit recalculations and Wakala claims take up to 20 business days, and never more than 30 working days in total. Unsatisfied customers escalate to Mal's Customer Advocacy Office for a further ten working days; Sharia questions go to the Internal Sharia Supervision Committee, whose determination binds Mal; the final external step is the UAE Central Bank consumer protection route, Sanadak.

**Why is my full account number hidden, and what will Mal never ask me for?**
Mal Digital Wakala account numbers display as `MAL-****-****-4417`, and the full number and IBAN are revealed only after in-app biometric re-authentication; they never appear in push notifications, SMS or email. Mal will never ask, through any channel, for a full account number, a full Emirates ID number, a password, a passcode, a one-time code or biometric data, and will never ask a customer to move money to a "safe account". Report fraud under Support → Report Fraud or on +971-4-000-0000; Mal freezes the account within two minutes.

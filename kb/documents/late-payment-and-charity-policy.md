# Mal Sharia-Compliant Late Payment and Charity Policy (Wujuh al-Khair)

Mal never charges interest, riba or compounding on a late or missed payment, and the amount a customer owes Mal never grows because of delay. The only charge that can apply is a flat AED 100 deterrent per missed instalment, capped over the life of an agreement at the lower of 1% of the outstanding amount or AED 500, of which one hundred percent is donated to charity through the Mal Wujuh al-Khair channel and no part is recognised as Mal income.

This Mal Sharia-Compliant Late Payment and Charity Policy explains the fixed no-interest rule, the flat charity-donated late payment charge, the 5 calendar day grace period and the day-by-day escalation sequence, catch-up payments, credit bureau reporting, hardship support and restructuring, waivers and technical payment failures, disputes and external escalation, deceased customers and estates, payment clearing, the narrow actual-cost exception, Sharia governance and conflict controls, worked arithmetic examples, how the policy applies to Mal Everyday Murabaha, Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek and Mal Tawarruq, a comparison with conventional late fees, a glossary and a frequently asked questions section. It is approved by the Mal Internal Sharia Supervision Committee (ISSC) and applies to every Mal retail customer.

## No Interest, Ever

Mal never charges interest, riba, compounding, or any increase in the outstanding amount because a payment is late. The amount a customer owes Mal on the day a payment is missed is the same amount owed thirty, ninety or three hundred days later. The debt is fixed at contract, and under the Mal Sharia-Compliant Late Payment and Charity Policy nothing in a delay can make it grow.

Increasing a fixed debt in exchange for time is riba al-nasi'ah, prohibited outright. Mal therefore has no default interest rate, no penalty profit rate, no rescheduling markup and no mechanism anywhere in its systems that recalculates a customer's principal or profit because of lateness.

Any charge a late customer sees in the Mal app is a separate, flat deterrent charge described in the next section. It is never added to the financed amount, never accrues, and never earns Mal a single dirham of income.

## The Late Payment Charge (Ta'widh and Gharamah)

Where an instalment remains unpaid after the grace period, Mal applies a flat late payment charge of **AED 100 per missed instalment**. Under the Mal Sharia-Compliant Late Payment and Charity Policy this charge is a gharamah — a deterrent designed to discourage avoidable delay by customers who are able to pay — and not a price for extra time.

The charge is capped. Across the whole life of a financing agreement, total late payment charges will not exceed **1% of the outstanding financed amount or AED 500, whichever is lower**. Once that cap is reached, no further late payment charges are applied to that agreement, regardless of how many further instalments are missed.

The charge does not scale with the size of the debt or the length of the delay. A customer who is 400 days late pays the same AED 100 for that instalment as a customer who is 6 days late.

## Where the Money Goes: Wujuh al-Khair

One hundred percent of every late payment charge collected by Mal is donated to UAE-approved charitable causes through the Mal Wujuh al-Khair channel. Mal does not recognise any part of it as revenue, and it does not appear in Mal's profit and loss statement in any form.

On collection, the charge is booked immediately to a segregated charity account held apart from Mal's operating funds. Balances there cannot be lent, invested for Mal's own account, used to offset Mal's costs, or drawn on by any Mal business unit. Disbursement is authorised only by the Mal Internal Sharia Supervision Committee.

Named beneficiaries under the Mal Wujuh al-Khair channel for the current cycle are the Bayt al-Rahma Family Relief Fund, the Nakhla Foundation for Student Fees, the Emirates Dialysis Support Trust, and the Sadaqat Shelter Initiative for Domestic Workers. Beneficiaries are reviewed annually and must hold a valid UAE charitable licence.

Because Mal earns nothing from late payments, Mal has no incentive to let a customer stay late. Mal collections staff are measured on customers returned to good standing, never on charges collected.

## Grace Period and Notification Sequence

Mal applies a **5 calendar day grace period** after every instalment due date. Payment received within those five days is treated as on time: no late payment charge, no credit bureau impact, and no change to the customer's Mal standing.

Mal notifies customers at every step through the Mal app, SMS and email, in English and Arabic. Notices state the exact amount due, the exact charge if any, and a one-tap link to pay. Mal makes no collection calls before day 15.

## How to Catch Up

Mal accepts partial payments at any time, in any amount, with no minimum and no fee. Partial payment slows escalation and is recorded immediately.

Mal allocates payments in a fixed order: oldest overdue instalment first, then newer overdue instalments in date order, then outstanding late payment charges, and finally future instalments. Charges are deliberately allocated last so customer money always reduces the actual debt first.

Every charge is visible in the Mal app under Payments → Charges, showing the date, amount, the instalment it relates to, and a Wujuh al-Khair tag confirming it went to the segregated charity account. Disputes can be raised in-app and the charge is suspended while under review. Collections queries go to collections@mal.example or +971-4-000-0000.

## Consequences of Continued Non-Payment

Mal reports account status to the Al Etihad Credit Bureau (AECB) as required of all UAE finance providers. Delinquency of 60 days or more is reported and affects the customer's credit score and their ability to obtain finance from any UAE institution, not only Mal.

From day 30, Mal blocks new financing applications and suspends unused facility limits. Existing agreements are unaffected in amount — again, the debt does not grow — but the customer cannot take on further obligations while in arrears.

Legal recourse is a last resort under the Mal Sharia-Compliant Late Payment and Charity Policy, used only where deliberate default is evidenced and all hardship routes have been offered and declined. Any Mal court claim seeks the outstanding contractual amount plus documented actual costs only — never interest or damages for delay.

## Genuine Hardship Support

A customer who cannot pay should tell Mal early. The Mal Sharia-Compliant Late Payment and Charity Policy treats early disclosure as cooperation, not as default, and hardship arrangements approved by Mal carry no late payment charge for the months they cover.

Mal offers three hardship routes: **deferral** of one or two instalments to the end of the schedule; **restructuring** to a longer tenor with smaller instalments; and a **payment holiday** of up to three months for verified job loss or serious medical events.

Restructuring under the Mal Wujuh al-Khair policy never increases the principal owed. Mal may lengthen the tenor and reduce the monthly amount, but the total sum a customer repays after restructuring is never more than the total agreed at contract. There is no restructuring fee and no rescheduling markup.

Evidence required is deliberately light: for job loss, a termination letter and final payslip; for medical hardship, a licensed clinic or hospital letter confirming the condition and expected recovery period; for business income loss, three months of bank statements. Apply in the Mal app under Help → Payment Support or write to support@mal.example. Mal targets a decision within two working days.

## Deliberate Default Versus Genuine Inability

Sharia draws a sharp line between the debtor who cannot pay and the debtor who can pay but chooses not to. The instruction toward one in genuine difficulty is forbearance — respite until ease. The one who is solvent and delays deliberately is the mumatil, the wrongful procrastinator, and it is that person the Mal deterrent charge is aimed at.

Where hardship is disclosed and evidenced, Mal grants respite, waives accrued late payment charges for the covered period, and works to a restructured plan. Where a customer with evident means ignores instalments and does not engage, the deterrent charge stands and escalation proceeds on the published timeline.

Mal never assumes bad faith. A customer is treated as a deliberate defaulter only after documented non-engagement across multiple contact attempts, and that classification is reversed at any point by contacting Mal with hardship evidence.

## Restructuring in Detail: What Changes and What Cannot

Restructuring under the Mal Sharia-Compliant Late Payment and Charity Policy is open to any customer in genuine difficulty and is the route Mal prefers over escalation. What Mal can change is the shape of the schedule: the tenor may be extended by up to 24 months beyond the original term, the monthly instalment reduced accordingly, the due date moved to align with a salary date, and one or two instalments moved to the end of the schedule.

What Mal cannot change is the money. The principal does not increase, the total sum repayable over the restructured schedule is never more than the total agreed at contract, and there is no restructuring fee, arrangement fee or rescheduling markup of any kind. The Mal Internal Sharia Supervision Committee's position is explicit: rescheduling a debt in exchange for any increase in the amount owed is riba al-nasi'ah, and no Mal system can produce a restructured schedule with a higher total than the original.

Documentation is a restructuring addendum signed in the Mal app, restating the unchanged total, setting out the new instalment amounts and dates, and confirming in writing that no markup was added. Mal targets a decision within two working days of a complete application and issues the addendum within one working day of approval. No late payment charge applies for any month covered by an approved restructuring, and charges applied in the months immediately before an approved hardship application are reviewed for waiver.

## Waivers, First-Time Forgiveness and Failed Direct Debits

Under the Mal Sharia-Compliant Late Payment and Charity Policy a customer may have one late payment charge waived at Mal's discretion once in any rolling 12 month period, on any Mal financing agreement, without giving a reason. Request it in the Mal app under Payments → Charges → Request Waiver, or write to collections@mal.example quoting the masked account number shown in the app, for example MAL-\***\*-\*\***-4417. Mal targets a decision within one working day. A waived Mal charge is cancelled outright: never reinstated later, never revived if the customer falls behind again, and never counted toward the lifetime cap, because nothing was collected and nothing reached the charity account.

Separately, and not at Mal's discretion, Mal applies no charge at all where the missed payment was caused by Mal or by the payment rail. That covers a direct debit Mal failed to present, a mandate Mal cancelled in error, a Mal app or core banking outage, a payment processed against the wrong due date, and a UAE clearing system disruption. Mal's payments platform reconciles presentments nightly and automatically reverses any charge linked to a presentment carrying a technical or Mal-side rejection code, normally within 24 hours and without the customer asking. Where the rejection code shows insufficient funds, the charge stands unless the customer disputes it with evidence.

## Disputing a Late Payment Charge and Escalating a Complaint

Any customer may dispute a Mal late payment charge, in the Mal app under Payments → Charges → Dispute or by email to collections@mal.example. Under the Mal Sharia-Compliant Late Payment and Charity Policy the disputed charge is suspended immediately: it stops being collectible, is excluded from the allocation order, and is not transferred to the Wujuh al-Khair charity account while review is open. Mal acknowledges within one working day and targets a decision within five working days, extendable to ten where a third-party payment provider must respond.

Mal considers bank statements showing a failed or returned direct debit, payment confirmations and reference numbers, screenshots of a Mal app failure, correspondence showing an agreed change of due date, and records of a hardship application filed before the due date. Outcomes are: charge cancelled, charge upheld with a written explanation, or charge cancelled with a process failure logged for internal audit. A cancelled charge leaves no residue on the account.

A dissatisfied customer may escalate. Mal's complaints team responds substantively within ten working days of a formal complaint to support@mal.example or +971-4-000-0000. Where the objection is that a charge is not Sharia-compliant, the matter goes to the Mal Internal Sharia Supervision Committee, which responds within 30 days and whose ruling binds Mal. The final step is the UAE Central Bank consumer protection route, and Mal supplies the case reference and file on request.

## The Deceased Customer and the Estate

The Mal Sharia-Compliant Late Payment and Charity Policy treats a customer's death as a full stop on charges. From the date of death no further late payment charge can arise on any Mal financing agreement held by that customer, and none accrues during estate settlement however long that takes. Mal applies this from the date of death itself, not the date Mal is notified, and reverses any charge applied in between.

On notification — which family members or an executor can give through support@mal.example or +971-4-000-0000 with a death certificate — Mal suspends collections activity, stops automated reminders, cancels scheduled direct debits and freezes escalation. Mal does not report a deceased customer as delinquent to the Al Etihad Credit Bureau and closes any open delinquency flag.

The outstanding amount remains a debt of the estate, settled from the estate under UAE law at the contractual amount with no increase for the passage of time. Mal contacts the estate representative rather than family members individually, does not pursue heirs personally beyond their liability under law, and considers write-off where the estate is demonstrably insufficient, subject to Mal Internal Sharia Supervision Committee approval. Where a Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek vehicle is involved, the representative may continue the lease, settle it early or return the vehicle, and Mal applies no charge for the time taken to decide.

## The Narrow Exception: Actual Cost Recovery

Sharia scholars permit a financier to recover ta'widh — real, evidenced, direct loss actually incurred in recovering a debt. This is distinct from the deterrent gharamah, and the Mal Sharia-Compliant Late Payment and Charity Policy keeps the two strictly separate.

Where Mal incurs actual third-party costs of collection — court filing fees, licensed process-server fees, or external legal counsel invoiced on a case — Mal may seek recovery of those documented amounts. Recovery is limited to the invoiced amount, requires the invoice to be on file, and requires case-by-case approval by the Mal Internal Sharia Supervision Committee.

Actual cost recovery is never estimated, never expressed as a percentage of the debt, and never applied automatically. It does not arise in ordinary retail delinquency, only in the small number of cases reaching litigation. Amounts recovered offset Mal's documented out-of-pocket expense and, unlike the gharamah, are not routed to the charity account — they reimburse a real cost rather than penalise the customer.

## How Mal Governs Against a Conflict of Interest

Because every dirham of Mal late payment charges goes to charity and none to Mal, Mal has no financial interest in a customer being late. The Mal Sharia-Compliant Late Payment and Charity Policy backs that structural fact with explicit controls, since a stated absence of incentive is worth little untested.

Mal collections staff have no targets, quotas or bonus components tied to late payment charges applied or collected, and setting such a target is a disciplinary matter under Mal's conduct rules. Collections performance is measured on customers returned to good standing, hardship arrangements agreed, complaint volumes and first-contact resolution. Waiver approvals are not rationed by budget, and a Mal Care agent's waiver approval rate is never used against them in performance review.

The charity account is segregated at ledger level from Mal operating funds, is unavailable to Mal treasury, cannot be pledged, lent or invested for Mal's own account, and is reconciled monthly by Mal Finance with second-reviewer sign-off. Only the Mal Internal Sharia Supervision Committee authorises disbursement. Mal Internal Audit tests the control set at least annually, sampling charge applications for correct grace period handling, sampling waiver and dispute decisions for consistency, and confirming that no charge revenue reached a profit and loss line. Findings, including any control failure, are disclosed in the annual Wujuh al-Khair report. Governance questions go to sharia@mal.example.

## Worked Example: One Missed Instalment

This example shows the arithmetic of one missed instalment under the Mal Sharia-Compliant Late Payment and Charity Policy. A customer holds a Mal Everyday Murabaha plan with a total sale price of AED 24,000 over 24 monthly instalments of AED 1,000. Instalment 7 falls due on 3 March and is not paid. The customer pays on 21 March — fifteen days past the due date, ten days past the end of the Mal grace period.

| Line                                        | Amount     |
| ------------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| Total sale price agreed at contract         | AED 24,000 |
| Paid before instalment 7                    | AED 6,000  |
| Outstanding before the missed instalment    | AED 18,000 |
| Increase to the debt from the 15 day delay  | AED 0      |
| Instalment 7 paid on 21 March               | AED 1,000  |
| Flat Mal late payment charge, applied day 6 | AED 100    |
| Portion of that AED 100 kept by Mal         | AED 0      |
| Portion donated through Wujuh al-Khair      | AED 100    |

The customer transfers AED 1,100. Mal allocates AED 1,000 to instalment 7 and AED 100 to the charge, in that order, because Mal always reduces the real debt first. The AED 100 is booked the same day to the segregated Wujuh al-Khair charity account and shows in the Mal app under Payments → Charges with a Wujuh al-Khair tag. The balance is then AED 17,000 across 17 instalments — exactly what it would have been had instalment 7 been paid on time.

## Worked Example: Repeated Misses and the Lifetime Cap

This example shows the Mal lifetime cap binding. A customer has AED 30,000 outstanding on a Mal financing agreement and misses six instalments over eleven months, each beyond the 5 calendar day grace period. Six misses at AED 100 would arithmetically be AED 600, but the cap under the Mal Sharia-Compliant Late Payment and Charity Policy is the lower of 1% of the outstanding amount (AED 300) or AED 500. AED 300 is lower, so AED 300 is the most this agreement can ever carry in late payment charges.

| Missed instalment | Arithmetically due | Actually applied    | Cumulative |
| ----------------- | ------------------ | ------------------- | ---------- |
| 1st               | AED 100            | AED 100             | AED 100    |
| 2nd               | AED 100            | AED 100             | AED 200    |
| 3rd               | AED 100            | AED 100             | AED 300    |
| 4th               | AED 100            | AED 0 — cap reached | AED 300    |
| 5th               | AED 100            | AED 0 — cap reached | AED 300    |
| 6th               | AED 100            | AED 0 — cap reached | AED 300    |

From the fourth miss onward Mal applies no further charge to that agreement for the rest of its life. The cap is not reset by restructuring, by a change of due date, or by the customer returning to good standing and later falling behind again. The AED 300 collected goes entirely to charity. Escalation steps such as the day 30 block on new finance and day 60 credit bureau reporting still apply, because those are risk measures rather than charges.

## How the Policy Applies to Mal Everyday Murabaha

Mal Everyday Murabaha is a cost-plus sale for goods and travel with a fixed sale price and a facility cap of AED 150,000. Because the customer's obligation is a fixed sale receivable created at the moment of sale, the Mal Sharia-Compliant Late Payment and Charity Policy applies to it in its simplest form: the total price cannot change, in either direction, for any reason connected to lateness.

If a Mal Everyday Murabaha instalment is unpaid after the 5 calendar day grace period, one flat AED 100 charge applies to that instalment. The charge sits outside the sale price on a separate charge line. It is never merged into the receivable, never re-amortised across the remaining instalments, and never recalculated as the delay lengthens — a customer 300 days late on one instalment owes the same AED 100 as a customer 6 days late.

The lifetime cap of the lower of 1% of outstanding or AED 500 is measured per Mal Everyday Murabaha agreement, so a customer with three separate plans has three separate caps. Early settlement creates no late payment charge and does not recover any charge already donated. Pricing, cancellation and returns are covered by the Mal Everyday Murabaha document rather than by this policy.

## How the Policy Applies to Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek

Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek is an auto lease-to-own arrangement in which Mal owns the vehicle until ownership transfers at the end of the lease. The Mal Sharia-Compliant Late Payment and Charity Policy applies to it, with one structural difference from Mal Everyday Murabaha that customers should understand.

Under Murabaha the customer owes a single debt fixed at contract. Under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek the customer owes rent for periods of actual use, and that rent has a fixed portion and a variable portion. Unpaid rent is therefore not a crystallised debt in the same way: rent for a period already used becomes payable and does not grow because it is late, while rent for future periods has not accrued at all. Missing a rental does not accelerate future rent into a lump sum under this Mal policy.

The deterrent charge itself works identically — AED 100 flat per missed rental after the 5 calendar day grace period, the same lifetime cap, donated in full through Wujuh al-Khair. What differs is Mal's remedy: because Mal is the legal owner of the vehicle, Mal's remedy for persistent non-payment includes recovery of the vehicle rather than only a money claim. Repossession triggers, notice periods, the customer's equity in accrued ownership instalments and the sale-proceeds settlement are set out in the Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek document.

## How the Policy Applies to Mal Tawarruq, and What It Does Not Cover

Mal Tawarruq is a narrow cash facility offered only where no asset-based alternative meets a genuine liquidity need. The Mal Sharia-Compliant Late Payment and Charity Policy applies to it on identical terms to every other Mal financing product: a 5 calendar day grace period, a flat AED 100 deterrent per missed instalment, the cap at the lower of 1% of outstanding or AED 500, full donation through Wujuh al-Khair, and no interest, compounding or increase in the amount owed. Mal deliberately does not charge more on Tawarruq — a charge that varied by product would look like a price for money and time rather than a deterrent. The same escalation table applies, and the same treatment extends to any future Mal credit product.

The policy governs credit products only. Mal Digital Wakala savings and Mal Fractional Sukuk investments are not credit products, and nothing here applies to them. An expected profit rate that is not achieved on a Mal Digital Wakala is a profit shortfall, not a late payment: it triggers no charge, no donation, no escalation and no credit bureau entry. A fall in the value of a Mal Fractional Sukuk holding, a delayed issuer distribution or a loss on secondary sale is an investment outcome governed by the Mal Fractional Sukuk document. Merchant disputes, optional service fees in the Mal fee schedule, and voluntary sadaqah made by customers in the Mal app are likewise outside this policy.

## Mal Compared With a Conventional Late Fee

Customers often ask how Mal differs from the late fee or default interest charged by a conventional bank. The Mal Sharia-Compliant Late Payment and Charity Policy differs on every material dimension, not merely in naming.

| Dimension                            | Conventional late payment treatment                     | Mal under the Wujuh al-Khair policy            |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Interest on arrears                  | Default rate applied to the overdue balance             | None — AED 0, never applied                    |
| Compounding                          | Unpaid interest capitalised and itself bearing interest | None — the debt cannot grow                    |
| Amount owed after 12 months of delay | Materially higher than the original balance             | Identical to the original balance              |
| Size of the charge                   | Often a percentage, so larger debts pay more            | Flat AED 100 per missed instalment             |
| Effect of a longer delay             | Grows with time outstanding                             | Unchanged whether 6 days or 400 days late      |
| Lifetime cap                         | Frequently none                                         | Lower of 1% of outstanding or AED 500          |
| Who keeps the money                  | The bank, as fee or interest income                     | Nobody at Mal — 100% donated to charity        |
| Accounting treatment                 | Revenue in the profit and loss statement                | Segregated charity liability, never Mal income |
| Staff incentives                     | Collections targets may include fee income              | Charge-based targets prohibited outright       |
| Purpose                              | Priced compensation for time and risk                   | Deterrent against avoidable delay only         |

The practical consequence is that a period of difficulty leaves a Mal customer's debt exactly where it was. A customer who owes Mal AED 18,000 and pays nothing for a year still owes AED 18,000, plus at most the capped deterrent charge that Mal itself never receives.

## Glossary and Related Mal Documents

These are the terms used in the Mal Sharia-Compliant Late Payment and Charity Policy. **Riba al-nasi'ah** is the prohibited increase in a debt given in exchange for extra time, and is why Mal charges no default interest. **Gharamah** is a deterrent charge meant to discourage avoidable delay rather than price it — what the Mal AED 100 charge is, and why Mal cannot keep it. **Ta'widh** is compensation for real, evidenced, direct loss actually incurred, recoverable by Mal only with an invoice on file and case-by-case ISSC approval. **Mumatil** is the solvent debtor who delays deliberately, the only person the Mal deterrent charge targets. **Wujuh al-Khair** means channels of good and names the Mal segregated charity account and disbursement process. **Purification** is the removal of impermissible amounts from income, achieved at Mal structurally by never recognising the charge as income. **Grace period** is the 5 calendar days after a due date during which a Mal payment is on time. **Restructuring** changes schedule shape only, never the total repayable. **AECB** is the Al Etihad Credit Bureau. **ISSC** is the Mal Internal Sharia Supervision Committee.

For product terms rather than late payment treatment, see the Mal Everyday Murabaha document for goods and travel financing, the Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek document for auto lease-to-own and repossession, the Mal Digital Wakala document for savings and expected profit rates, the Mal Fractional Sukuk document for investment and capital risk, and the Mal Tawarruq document for the cash facility.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Will I be charged interest if I miss a payment?**
No. Mal never charges interest for a late or missed payment, and the amount you owe Mal never increases because of a delay. There is no default interest rate, no compounding and no penalty profit at Mal. What may apply is a single flat late payment charge of AED 100 for the missed instalment, applied only after a 5 calendar day grace period, and 100% of that charge is donated to charity through the Mal Wujuh al-Khair channel rather than kept by Mal.

**How much is the late payment charge and is there a limit?**
The Mal late payment charge is a flat AED 100 per missed instalment. Total late payment charges on any single Mal financing agreement are capped at 1% of the outstanding amount or AED 500, whichever is lower, after which no further charges apply.

**Where does my late payment charge actually go?**
Every dirham goes to UAE-approved charities through the Mal Wujuh al-Khair channel, including the Bayt al-Rahma Family Relief Fund, the Nakhla Foundation for Student Fees, the Emirates Dialysis Support Trust and the Sadaqat Shelter Initiative for Domestic Workers. The money is booked to a segregated charity account, cannot be recognised as Mal revenue, and disbursement is authorised and published annually by the Mal Internal Sharia Supervision Committee.

**I have lost my job and cannot pay this month. What are my options?**
Tell Mal before the due date through Help → Payment Support in the Mal app or at support@mal.example. Mal can defer instalments, arrange a payment holiday of up to three months, or restructure to a longer tenor with smaller instalments. Approved hardship arrangements carry no late payment charge, and restructuring at Mal never increases the principal you owe.

**Does missing a payment affect my credit record?**
Yes, if it continues. Mal reports accounts that are 60 days or more overdue to the Al Etihad Credit Bureau, and Mal blocks new financing applications from day 30. Paying within the 5 day grace period has no credit impact at all, and agreeing a hardship plan with Mal before day 60 avoids delinquent reporting.

**Can Mal ever charge me more than the AED 100 deterrent?**
Only in the narrow case of actual, evidenced direct costs of collection — such as court filing fees or invoiced external legal fees — which Mal may seek to recover with case-by-case Internal Sharia Supervision Committee approval and the invoice on file. That is reimbursement of a real cost, is limited to the documented amount, does not arise in ordinary retail arrears, and is never a percentage of your debt.

**My direct debit failed and it was not my fault. Will Mal still charge me?**
No. Mal applies no late payment charge where the missed payment was caused by Mal or by the payment rail — a direct debit Mal failed to present, a mandate cancelled in error, a Mal app or core banking outage, a payment processed against the wrong due date, or a UAE clearing disruption. Mal reconciles presentments nightly and reverses such charges automatically, normally within 24 hours and without you asking. If the rejection code shows insufficient funds the charge stands, but you can still dispute it with evidence at collections@mal.example.

**Can I get a late payment charge waived?**
Yes, once in any rolling 12 month period Mal will waive a late payment charge at its discretion, on any Mal financing agreement, without requiring a reason. Request it in the Mal app under Payments → Charges → Request Waiver or email collections@mal.example, and Mal targets a decision within one working day. A waived Mal charge is cancelled outright — it is never reinstated later, never revived if you fall behind again, and never counted toward the AED 500 lifetime cap.

**How do I dispute a Mal late payment charge?**
Raise the dispute in the Mal app under Payments → Charges → Dispute or email collections@mal.example. Mal suspends the charge immediately, so it is not collectible and is not sent to the charity account while the review is open. Mal acknowledges within one working day and targets a decision within five working days, extendable to ten if a third-party payment provider must respond. Evidence Mal considers includes failed direct debit records, payment references, app failure screenshots and an agreed due date change. Outcomes are cancellation, or upholding with a written explanation.

**What happens if I miss a rental payment on my Mal car lease?**
Under Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek the same flat AED 100 charge, 5 calendar day grace period and lifetime cap apply, and the charge is donated in full through Wujuh al-Khair. The difference is that Ijara rent accrues for periods of actual use rather than being a single fixed debt, so missing one rental does not accelerate future rent into a lump sum. Because Mal owns the vehicle until ownership transfers, Mal's remedy for persistent non-payment includes recovery of the vehicle; those terms are in the Mal Ijara Muntahia Bittamleek document.

**How is the Mal late payment charge different from a normal bank's late fee?**
A conventional bank typically applies default interest to the overdue balance, compounds it, sizes the fee as a percentage of the debt, often caps nothing, and books the money as revenue. Mal applies no interest and no compounding, so the amount owed after twelve months of delay is identical to the amount owed on day one. The Mal charge is a flat AED 100 per missed instalment regardless of the debt's size or the delay's length, capped over the agreement's life at the lower of 1% of outstanding or AED 500, and one hundred percent of it is donated to charity rather than kept by Mal.

**What happens to a Mal financing agreement if the customer dies?**
No late payment charge can arise from the date of death, and none accrues at any point during estate settlement however long it takes. Mal applies this from the date of death rather than the date of notification and reverses charges applied in between. On notification with a death certificate to support@mal.example, Mal suspends collections, cancels direct debits, stops escalation and does not report the customer as delinquent to the Al Etihad Credit Bureau. The outstanding contractual amount remains a debt of the estate, unchanged in size, and Mal considers write-off with ISSC approval where the estate is demonstrably insufficient.

**Does this policy apply to my Mal savings or investments?**
No. The Mal Sharia-Compliant Late Payment and Charity Policy covers credit products only. Mal Digital Wakala savings and Mal Fractional Sukuk investments are not credit products, so a profit shortfall against an expected rate, a fall in sukuk value or a delayed issuer distribution is never a late payment, never triggers a charge or a Wujuh al-Khair donation, and never appears on your credit record. Those outcomes are governed by the Mal Digital Wakala and Mal Fractional Sukuk documents.
